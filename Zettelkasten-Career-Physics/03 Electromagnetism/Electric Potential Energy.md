# Electric Potential Energy

Just as gravity has an associated potential energy, so does the electric force — since both are conservative forces (the work done moving a charge between two points doesn't depend on the path taken).

For two point charges $q_1, q_2$ separated by distance $r$:

$$
PE = k_e\frac{q_1 q_2}{r}
$$

Positive for like charges (energy would be released as they fly apart, since they repel) and negative for opposite charges (energy would be released as they come together, since they attract) — the sign convention mirrors the direction the system "wants" to move.

## Electric potential (voltage)

Dividing potential energy by the test charge gives **electric potential** $V = PE/q$, measured in volts — a property of the field itself (like $\vec{E}$), independent of whatever charge you place there to measure it. This is the quantity meant by "voltage" in circuits: the potential *difference* between two points is what drives current to flow between them, in direct analogy to how a height difference drives water to flow downhill.

This concept underlies the behaviour of **capacitors** (see [[Capacitors and Inductors]]), which store energy precisely as electric potential energy in the field between charged plates.
