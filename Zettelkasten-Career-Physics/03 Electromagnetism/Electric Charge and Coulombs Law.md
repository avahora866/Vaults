# Electric Charge and Coulomb's Law

**Electric charge** is a fundamental property of matter, coming in two types conventionally called positive and negative. Charge is quantized (it always comes in integer multiples of the elementary charge $e$) and conserved (the total charge in an isolated system never changes). Like charges repel; opposite charges attract.

## Coulomb's law

The force between two point charges $q_1$ and $q_2$ separated by distance $r$ is

$$
F = k_e\frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}, \qquad k_e \approx 8.99\times10^9\ \text{N·m}^2/\text{C}^2
$$

directed along the line joining them — repulsive for like charges, attractive for opposite ones. Note the inverse-square form, the same mathematical shape as Newton's law of gravitation, but electromagnetic forces are vastly stronger than gravity at the scale of individual particles.

## The electric field

Rather than computing the force between two charges directly, it's often more useful to describe the **electric field** $\vec{E}$ that a charge (or distribution of charges) creates in the surrounding space:

$$
\vec{E} = \frac{\vec{F}}{q}
$$

A second, "test" charge $q$ placed anywhere in this field then feels a force $\vec{F} = q\vec{E}$. This field-based picture is what generalises cleanly to changing charge distributions and, eventually, to electromagnetic waves — a field can carry influence through empty space even where there's no matter to exert or feel a direct force.

For a single point charge $Q$, the field it creates at distance $r$ is

$$
E = k_e\frac{Q}{r^2}
$$

pointing radially outward (for positive $Q$) or inward (for negative $Q$).
