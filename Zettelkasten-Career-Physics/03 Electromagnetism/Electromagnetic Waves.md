# Electromagnetic Waves

James Clerk Maxwell's equations show that a changing electric field generates a magnetic field, and a changing magnetic field generates an electric field. This mutual generation allows the two fields to sustain each other and propagate through space as a self-contained wave — an **electromagnetic wave** — even through a vacuum, with no medium required.

## Structure and speed

An electromagnetic wave consists of oscillating $\vec{E}$ and $\vec{B}$ fields, perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation (making it a transverse wave — see [[02 Waves and Oscillations]]). All electromagnetic waves travel at the same speed in vacuum, the speed of light:

$$
c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}} \approx 3\times10^8\ \text{m/s}
$$

related to wavelength and frequency the same way as any wave, $c = f\lambda$.

## The electromagnetic spectrum

**Light** — visible or otherwise — is just an electromagnetic wave. The **electromagnetic spectrum** spans, in order of increasing frequency (decreasing wavelength): radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays. All are the same physical phenomenon; only the wavelength (and correspondingly the photon energy — see [[Plancks Hypothesis]]) differs.

## Polarization

Because $\vec{E}$ (and $\vec{B}$) oscillate in directions perpendicular to propagation, there's a further degree of freedom: *which* perpendicular direction the electric field oscillates in. This is **polarization** — see [[Polarization]] for details, including why it matters for understanding photon behaviour in quantum contexts.

## Why this matters

Recognising light as an electromagnetic wave unified optics with electricity and magnetism into a single theory — one of the great triumphs of 19th-century physics. It's also the direct classical predecessor to quantum electrodynamics: when this same wave is examined at very low intensity, it turns out to deliver energy in discrete packets (photons) rather than continuously, one of the central puzzles addressed in [[Wave-Particle Duality]].
