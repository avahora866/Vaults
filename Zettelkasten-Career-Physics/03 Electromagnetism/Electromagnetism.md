# Electromagnetism

**Electromagnetism** is the study of electric charge, the electric and magnetic fields charges create and respond to, and — unifying the two — electromagnetic waves, including light itself. It is one of the four fundamental forces of nature, and the one responsible for essentially all of chemistry, everyday materials, and electrical/electronic engineering.

## Electric charge and fields

Electric charge comes in two types (positive and negative); like charges repel, opposite charges attract. **Coulomb's law** quantifies the force between two point charges:

$$
F = k_e\frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}
$$

Rather than thinking of charges acting on each other directly at a distance, it's more powerful to say each charge creates an **electric field** $\vec{E}$ in the space around it, and a second charge feels a force $\vec{F} = q\vec{E}$ due to that field. See [[Electric Charge and Coulombs Law]].

## Magnetism

**Magnetic fields** are produced by moving charges (currents) and by certain materials (permanent magnets, themselves ultimately due to aligned atomic-scale currents and spins). A moving charge in a magnetic field feels a force perpendicular to both its velocity and the field — the basis of electric motors. See [[Magnetic Fields and Moving Charges]].

## Electromagnetic waves

James Clerk Maxwell showed that changing electric fields generate magnetic fields and vice versa, and that this interplay allows disturbances to propagate through space as **electromagnetic waves** — self-sustaining oscillations of $\vec{E}$ and $\vec{B}$ perpendicular to each other and to the direction of travel, moving at the speed of light $c$. Visible light, radio waves, X-rays, and microwaves are all electromagnetic waves, differing only in wavelength/frequency. See [[Electromagnetic Waves]] and [[Polarization]].

## Energy in fields

Electric and magnetic fields themselves store energy — a charged capacitor or a current-carrying inductor holds real, recoverable energy in its field, not just in the charges or currents themselves. See [[Energy in Electric and Magnetic Fields]].

## Why this matters broadly

Electromagnetism underlies essentially all electrical and electronic engineering: motors and generators (moving charges in magnetic fields), circuits (charge, potential, capacitors, inductors), sensors, and wireless communication (electromagnetic waves). It's also the classical theory whose quantization — treating the electromagnetic field itself as made of discrete photons — was one of the original motivations for [[Modern Physics|quantum theory]].
