# Polarization

**Polarization** describes the orientation of the oscillating electric field in a transverse electromagnetic wave. Since $\vec{E}$ oscillates perpendicular to the direction of travel, it can point in any direction within that perpendicular plane — and how it does so (or how it varies) is the wave's polarization state.

* **Linear polarization**: $\vec{E}$ oscillates along a single fixed direction.
* **Circular/elliptical polarization**: the direction of $\vec{E}$ rotates as the wave propagates, tracing a circle or ellipse.
* **Unpolarized light** (like ordinary sunlight): a rapid, random mix of all polarization directions, with no single orientation dominating.

A **polarizing filter** only transmits the component of $\vec{E}$ aligned with its transmission axis, which is why polarized sunglasses reduce glare (reflected light off horizontal surfaces tends to be horizontally polarized) and why two polarizing filters at 90° to each other block essentially all light.

## Why polarization matters for quantum behaviour

Polarization turns out to be an unusually clean, accessible example of a genuinely **quantum two-level system**: a single photon's polarization state can be measured only as "aligned" or "not aligned" with a given filter axis, with probabilities set by the angle between the photon's polarization and the filter — direct evidence of quantum measurement and superposition playing out with a physically intuitive, everyday quantity, rather than something abstract. This is part of why polarization experiments are a standard way of demonstrating quantum superposition and entanglement in the lab. See [[Quantum States]].
