# Planck's Hypothesis

To correctly explain the spectrum of light radiated by a hot object (**blackbody radiation**) — something classical physics got badly wrong at high frequencies — Max Planck proposed in 1900 that energy is exchanged between radiation and matter only in discrete packets, or **quanta**, rather than continuously. Each quantum of radiation at frequency $f$ carries energy

$$
E = hf
$$

where $h \approx 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\ \text{J·s}$ is **Planck's constant** — now recognised as one of the fundamental constants of nature, setting the scale at which quantum effects become significant.

Planck initially treated this as a mathematical trick to fix the blackbody spectrum's formula, without believing energy was *literally* quantized. It was Einstein, five years later, who took the idea seriously as a physical statement about light itself to explain the [[Photoelectric Effect|photoelectric effect]] — proposing that light doesn't just exchange energy in quanta with matter, but *consists of* quanta (photons) carrying $E=hf$ each.

Planck's constant $h$ appears everywhere in quantum mechanics — it sets the scale of the [[Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle|uncertainty principle]], the [[De Broglie Matter Waves|de Broglie wavelength]], and the energy spacing of the [[Quantum Harmonic Oscillator|quantum harmonic oscillator]]. Its tiny value is exactly why quantum effects are invisible in everyday, macroscopic life but dominant at atomic scales.
