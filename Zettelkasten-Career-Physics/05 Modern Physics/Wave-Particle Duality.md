# Wave-Particle Duality

Classical physics treats waves and particles as fundamentally different things: particles are localized, discrete objects with definite trajectories; waves are spread-out disturbances that interfere and diffract. **Wave-particle duality** is the discovery that, at the quantum scale, this distinction breaks down — light and matter both display wave-like and particle-like behaviour, depending on what kind of experiment you perform.

## Light as a particle: the photoelectric effect

Light shone on certain metals ejects electrons — but only above a threshold *frequency*, regardless of the light's intensity, and the ejected electrons' energy depends on that frequency, not the intensity. This is inexplicable if light is a continuous wave (a bright enough low-frequency wave should eventually eject electrons, given enough time and energy) but falls out immediately if light delivers its energy in discrete packets — **photons** — each carrying energy $E = hf$. See [[Photoelectric Effect]].

## Matter as a wave: the de Broglie hypothesis

Louis de Broglie proposed the converse: if light (a wave) can behave like particles, perhaps particles (like electrons) can behave like waves, with an associated wavelength

$$
\lambda = \frac{h}{p}
$$

inversely proportional to momentum. This was confirmed experimentally — electrons produce interference patterns just like light does, when fired through a double slit. See [[De Broglie Matter Waves]].

## Resolving the apparent contradiction

The modern resolution isn't that light or matter are "sometimes waves, sometimes particles" arbitrarily — it's that both are described by a single quantum object (a wavefunction, or more generally a quantum state) that spreads and interferes like a wave, but which interacts with detectors and delivers energy/momentum in discrete, particle-like chunks. Which aspect you see depends on how you probe the system — this is formalised much more precisely in [[Quantum States]] and the [[Schrodinger Equation and State Evolution|Schrödinger equation]].
