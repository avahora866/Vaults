# Two-Level Systems

A **two-level system** is any quantum system where only two states are physically relevant — either because the system genuinely only has two possible states, or because higher-energy states are so much higher in energy that they can safely be ignored for the situation at hand. Despite their simplicity, two-level systems are one of the most important building blocks in quantum mechanics, because their mathematics is simple enough to solve exactly while still capturing genuinely quantum behaviour like superposition and entanglement.

## Canonical examples

* **Spin-½ particles** (like electrons): a measurement of spin along any chosen axis yields only one of two outcomes, conventionally called "spin up" and "spin down" — regardless of what direction is chosen, there are never more than two possible outcomes for that measurement.
* **Photon polarization**: as discussed in [[Polarization]], a photon's polarization along a chosen axis is measured as one of two outcomes (aligned or not).
* **An atom's ground and first excited energy state**, when higher excited states are far enough away in energy to be safely ignored for the interaction under consideration.

## Why two-level systems matter

A general two-level state is written as a superposition,

$$
|\psi\rangle = \alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle, \qquad |\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2=1
$$

exactly the superposition structure introduced in [[Quantum States]]. Physically realising and controlling a stable, well-isolated two-level system is the starting point for quantum information technologies — a two-level system encodes exactly the amount of quantum information referred to (in the more applied, engineering side of the subject) as a "qubit." That applied, hardware-and-computation layer is deliberately out of scope for this foundations-level note, but the physical two-level system itself — spins, polarizations, atomic energy levels — is squarely a physics concept, and understanding it here is the natural stepping-off point toward that more applied material later.
