# Mechanics

**Mechanics** is the branch of physics that describes how and why objects move. It splits into two questions: *how* something moves, independent of cause — **kinematics** — and *why* it moves, in terms of forces and energy — **dynamics**. Nearly every other branch of physics — electromagnetism, thermodynamics, quantum mechanics — reuses the vocabulary built in mechanics: position, velocity, force, energy, momentum, oscillation.

## Kinematics: describing motion

Kinematics gives the language for describing motion — position, velocity, acceleration — without asking what causes it. See [[02 Kinematics|Kinematics]] for the full treatment, including the standard kinematic equations and motion in more than one dimension.

## Dynamics: explaining motion

Dynamics explains motion in terms of forces (Newton's laws), momentum, energy and work, and the oscillatory motion that emerges from restoring forces (simple harmonic motion, resonance). See [[02 Dynamics|Dynamics]] for the full treatment.

## Why this matters beyond physics

Mechanics is the direct ancestor of engineering statics, dynamics, and control theory. Robotics in particular leans on Newton's second law (computing the forces needed to produce a desired acceleration), energy methods (for stability and efficiency), and harmonic motion (vibration, resonance, and oscillatory control systems).
