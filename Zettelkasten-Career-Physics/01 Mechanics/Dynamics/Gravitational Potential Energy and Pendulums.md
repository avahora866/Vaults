# Gravitational Potential Energy and Pendulums

**Gravitational potential energy** near Earth's surface is

$$
PE = mgh
$$

where $h$ is height above some reference level. It represents the work that would be done by gravity if the object fell from height $h$ to the reference level — energy "stored" by virtue of position in a gravitational field.

## The pendulum as a case study

A simple pendulum — a mass on a string, displaced and released — is one of the clearest illustrations of energy conservation in mechanics. At the highest point of its swing, the pendulum is momentarily at rest: all its mechanical energy is potential ($KE = 0$, $PE$ maximal). At the lowest point, it moves fastest: all that energy has converted to kinetic ($PE$ minimal, $KE$ maximal). At every point in between, the sum $KE + PE$ stays constant (ignoring air resistance and friction at the pivot).

For small swing angles, a pendulum's motion is approximately **simple harmonic motion** (see [[Simple Harmonic Motion]]), with period

$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}
$$

depending only on the pendulum's length $L$ and the local gravitational acceleration $g$ — notably *not* on the mass or the amplitude of swing (for small angles). This independence from mass is itself a consequence of the equivalence of inertial and gravitational mass (see [[Inertia and Mass]]).
