# Power

**Power** is the rate at which energy is transferred or work is done:

$$
P = \frac{dW}{dt}
$$

measured in watts (1 W = 1 joule/second). For a constant force moving an object at velocity $v$ in the direction of the force,

$$
P = Fv
$$

**Power vs. energy** is a common point of confusion: energy (joules) is the total amount transferred; power (watts) is how fast it's being transferred. A small motor can lift a heavy weight, given enough time — but it will do so at low power, taking a long time to deliver the same energy a more powerful motor could deliver quickly.

This distinction matters enormously in engineering: an electric motor is rated by power (how much work it can do per second), and matching motor power to a required task — accounting for both the energy needed and the time available to deliver it — is a routine part of mechanical and robotics design.
