# Momentum and Impulse

**Momentum** is a measure of "quantity of motion":

$$
\vec{p} = m\vec{v}
$$

It's a vector, pointing in the direction of velocity. Newton's second law can be written directly in terms of it:

$$
\vec{F}_{net} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt}
$$

— force is the rate of change of momentum. This is more general than $F=ma$ because it still holds when mass changes over time (rockets, for instance).

**Impulse** is the change in momentum produced by a force acting over some time interval:

$$
\vec{J} = \int \vec{F}\,dt = \Delta \vec{p}
$$

A small force applied for a long time can produce the same momentum change as a large force applied briefly — this is the whole idea behind crumple zones and airbags: they extend the time of a collision to reduce the peak force.

## Conservation of momentum

In a system with no net external force, total momentum is conserved — it doesn't change over time, even though momentum can be transferred between the objects inside the system (e.g. during a collision). This is one of the most powerful tools in mechanics precisely because it holds regardless of the messy details of *how* two objects interact during a collision.
