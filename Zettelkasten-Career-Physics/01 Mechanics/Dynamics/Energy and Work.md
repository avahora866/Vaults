# Energy and Work

Energy is one of the unifying concepts of all physics: it comes in many forms, converts between them, and (in an isolated system) is always conserved. In mechanics, the two central forms are kinetic and potential energy, linked by the concept of work.

## Work

**Work** is done when a force causes displacement:

$$
W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d} = Fd\cos\theta
$$

where $\theta$ is the angle between the force and the direction of motion. A force perpendicular to motion (like the normal force on a sliding block) does no work at all.

## Kinetic energy

**Kinetic energy** is the energy of motion:

$$
KE = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2
$$

## The work-energy theorem

The net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy:

$$
W_{net} = \Delta KE
$$

This is a direct consequence of Newton's second law, and it's often a much faster route to a problem's answer than tracking forces and accelerations step by step. See [[Work-Energy Theorem]].

## Potential energy

**Potential energy** is stored energy associated with position or configuration, defined for *conservative* forces (forces where the work done doesn't depend on the path taken — gravity and the spring force both qualify; friction does not). Near Earth's surface, gravitational potential energy is

$$
PE = mgh
$$

and for a spring stretched or compressed by $x$ from equilibrium,

$$
PE_{spring} = \tfrac{1}{2}kx^2
$$

## Conservation of mechanical energy

When only conservative forces act, total mechanical energy is conserved:

$$
KE + PE = \text{constant}
$$

A pendulum swinging, or a ball rolling down a frictionless hill, trades kinetic for potential energy and back, but the sum never changes. See [[Gravitational Potential Energy and Pendulums]]. Where non-conservative forces like friction or air resistance are present, mechanical energy isn't conserved on its own — it's converted into heat, sound, etc., and *total* energy (mechanical plus these other forms) is still conserved.

## Power

**Power** measures how quickly energy is transferred or work is done — see [[Power]].
