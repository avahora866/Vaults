# Inertia and Mass

**Inertia** is an object's tendency to resist changes in its state of motion — to keep doing what it's already doing unless something forces it to do otherwise. This is the content of Newton's first law.

**Mass** is the quantitative measure of inertia: the more mass an object has, the more force is required to produce a given acceleration. This is visible directly in Newton's second law, $\vec{F} = m\vec{a}$ — for a fixed force, a larger mass means a smaller acceleration.

Note the distinction between **inertial mass** (resistance to acceleration, as it appears in $F=ma$) and **gravitational mass** (how strongly an object is pulled by gravity, as it appears in $F = \frac{GMm}{r^2}$). Experimentally these are equal to extraordinary precision — a fact so fundamental that it became one of the starting points for Einstein's general theory of relativity (the equivalence principle).

Mass should not be confused with **weight**: weight is the force of gravity on an object ($W = mg$) and depends on location (you weigh less on the Moon), while mass is an intrinsic property of the object itself and doesn't change.
