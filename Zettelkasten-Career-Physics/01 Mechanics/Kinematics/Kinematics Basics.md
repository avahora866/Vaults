# Kinematics Basics

**Kinematics** describes motion — position, velocity, acceleration — without reference to the forces causing it. It's the vocabulary Newton's laws are written in.

* **Position** $x(t)$ (or $\vec{r}(t)$ in more than one dimension): where an object is, as a function of time.
* **Velocity** $v = \dfrac{dx}{dt}$: the rate of change of position. A vector — it has direction as well as magnitude (speed is just its magnitude).
* **Acceleration** $a = \dfrac{dv}{dt} = \dfrac{d^2x}{dt^2}$: the rate of change of velocity.

For **constant acceleration** (the most common introductory case — e.g. free fall under gravity), these relate through the standard kinematic equations:

$$
v = v_0 + at, \qquad x = x_0 + v_0 t + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2, \qquad v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a(x - x_0)
$$

In more than one dimension, motion in each direction is independent — a projectile's horizontal and vertical motion can be analysed separately, then recombined as vectors. This is the same idea underlying [[Orthoganol Components]].

Kinematics tells you *what* the motion looks like; Newton's second law ($F = ma$, see [[Newtons Laws of Motion]]) tells you *why* — by connecting the acceleration to the forces producing it.
