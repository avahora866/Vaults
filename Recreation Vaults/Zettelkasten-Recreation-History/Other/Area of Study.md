History is a wide field of inquiry encompassing many branches. Some branches focus on a specific time period, while others concentrate on a particular geographic region or a distinct theme
# Period
Chronological division is a common approach to organizing the vast expanse of history into more manageable segments. Different [periods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodization "Periodization") are often defined based on dominant themes that characterize a specific time frame and significant events that initiated these developments or brought them to an end. Depending on the selected context and level of detail, a period may be as short as a decade or longer than several centuries
A traditionally influential approach divides [human history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_history "Human history") into [prehistory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory "Prehistory"), [ancient history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_history "Ancient history"), [post-classical history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-classical_history "Post-classical history"), [early modern history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_history "Early modern history"), and [modern history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_history "Modern history")
# Geographical Location
Areas of historical study can also be categorized by the geographic locations they examine. [Geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography "Geography") plays a central role in history through its influence on [food production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_production "Food production"), [natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resources "Natural resources"), economic activities, political boundaries, and cultural interactions
Some historical works limit their scope to small regions, such as a village or a settlement. Others focus on broad territories that encompass entire continents, like the histories of Africa, Asia, Europe, the Americas, and Oceania

# Theme
Historians often limit their inquiry to a specific theme. Some propose a general subdivision into three major themes: [political history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_history "Political history"), [economic history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_history "Economic history"), and [social history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_history "Social history"). However, the boundaries between these branches are vague and their relation to other thematic branches, such as [intellectual history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_history "Intellectual history"), is not always clear
## Political
Political history studies the organization of [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_\(social_and_political\) "Power (social and political)") in society, examining how power structures arise, develop, and interact
[Diplomatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_history "Diplomatic history") and [military history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history "Military history") are associated with political history. Diplomatic history examines [international relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations "International relations") between states. It covers [foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy "Foreign policy") topics such as negotiations, strategic considerations, [treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaties "Treaties"), and conflicts between nations as well as the role of [international organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_organizations "International organizations") in these processes. Military history studies the impact and development of [armed conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_conflict "Armed conflict") in human history. This includes the examination of specific events, like the analysis of a particular battle and the discussion of the different causes of a war.
## Economic
Economic history examines how [commodities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodities "Commodities") are produced, exchanged, and consumed. It covers economic aspects such as the use of land, [labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Work_\(human_activity\) "Work (human activity)"), and [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(economics\) "Capital (economics)"), the [supply and demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_and_demand "Supply and demand") of goods, the costs and [means of production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Means_of_production "Means of production"), and the [distribution of income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_of_income "Distribution of income") and [wealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_of_wealth "Distribution of wealth"). Economic historians typically focus on general trends in the form of impersonal forces, such as [inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation "Inflation"), rather than the actions and decisions of individuals. If enough data is available, they rely on quantitative methods, like statistical analysis

## Social
Social history is a broad field investigating social phenomena, but its precise definition is disputed. Some theorists understand it as the study of everyday life outside the domains of politics and economics, including cultural practices, family structures, community interactions, and education. A closely related approach focuses on experience rather than activities, examining how members of particular social groups, like [social classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_class "Social class"), [races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(human_categorization\) "Race (human categorization)"), [genders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender "Gender"), or [age groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_group "Age group"), experienced their world. Other definitions see social history as the study of social problems, like poverty, disease, and crime, or take a broader perspective by examining how whole [societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Societies "Societies") developed.[[122]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History#cite_note-137) Closely related fields include [cultural history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_history "Cultural history"), [gender history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_history "Gender history"), and [religious history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_history "Religious history").
