**Chronological dating**, or simply **dating**, is the process of attributing to an object or event a date in the past, allowing such object or event to be located in a previously established [chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronology "Chronology")
This usually requires what is commonly known as a "dating method". Several dating methods exist like: history, archaeology, geology, palaeontology, astronomy, forensic science (investigating when the death of a cadaver occurred)
# Absolute vs Relative Dating Methods
Relative dating are unable to define specific dates but can define the impossibility of a particular event occurring before or after another event

Historians, for example, know that Shakespeare's play _[Henry V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_V_\(play\) "Henry V (play)")_ was not written before 1587 because Shakespeare's primary source for writing his play was the second edition of [Raphael Holinshed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael_Holinshed "Raphael Holinshed")'s _Chronicles_, not published until 1587.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronological_dating#cite_note-2) Thus, 1587 is the _post quem_ dating of Shakespeare's play _Henry V_. That means that the play was without fail written after (in Latin, _post_) 1587.

Absolute dating seeks to establish a specific date during which an object originates or an event occurs
