# What Are Planes?  
In drawing, **planes** refer to the flat surfaces that make up a 3D object. Each plane faces a different direction and interacts with light uniquely. Breaking down a complex form into **simplified planes** helps in understanding how light and shadow behave on a subject.

---

# How Planes Affect Values

Each plane receives a different amount of light based on its **angle relative to the light source**. This creates **value variation**, which gives the illusion of depth.

- **Planes facing the light** → **Brightest (Highlights)**
- **Planes angled slightly away** → **Midtones**
- **Planes turned further away** → **Core Shadows**
- **Planes blocked from light** → **Deep Shadows**
- **Planes catching bounced light** → **Reflected Light (Soft Glow in Shadows)**

---

# Planes in Different Forms

## Planes in Geometric Shapes

1. **Cube** – The simplest example: each face of the cube represents a single plane, with clear value shifts.
    
    - Top plane (if lit from above) → **Lightest**
    - Side planes → **Midtones**
    - Bottom (facing away from light) → **Darkest**
2. **Sphere** – Instead of distinct planes, a sphere has a **gradual transition** of values from light to shadow.
    
3. **Cylinders & Cones** – These have **curved planes**, meaning value shifts smoothly around the form.
    

## Planes in the Human Figure

1. **Face Planes** – The human face is often simplified into **planes** to understand light behavior:
    
    - **Forehead, nose bridge, cheekbones** → Catch the most light
    - **Eye sockets, under the nose, jawline** → Midtones and core shadows
    - **Neck, under the chin** → Deepest shadows
2. **Body & Limbs** – Muscles and bones create subtle plane changes, affecting light placement.

# Using Planes to Render Values Correctly

✅ **Identify the Light Source** – Determine where light is coming from to assign correct values.  
✅ **Break Complex Forms into Simple Planes** – Squint to see major value shifts.  
✅ **Start with Big Value Groups** – Large light and shadow areas before small details.  
✅ **Use Hard & Soft Edges** – Defined edges where planes change abruptly, soft where they curve.  
✅ **Reflected Light Consideration** – Don’t make shadows too flat; add subtle bounce light.

# How Light Interacts with the Body’s Planes

The way planes receive light depends on the **light source direction**:

✅ **Top-Down Light (Sunlight, Overhead Lights)**

- **Top-facing planes** (shoulders, top of head, top of arms) get **highlights**.
- **Side planes** (ribs, outer arms) get **midtones**.
- **Bottom planes** (under chin, under arms, under legs) fall into **shadows**.

✅ **Side Light**

- One side of the body gets **strong highlights**.
- The middle area has a **gradual transition**.
- The opposite side falls into **core shadow** with slight **reflected light** from surroundings.

✅ **Backlighting**

- The edges of the body have **rim lighting**.
- The front planes are mostly **in shadow**.
- Soft **reflected light** may appear on the torso and limbs.
