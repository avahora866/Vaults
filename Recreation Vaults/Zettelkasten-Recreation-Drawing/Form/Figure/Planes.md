### **Planes on the Human Body & How They Affect Values**

Understanding the planes of the human body helps simplify complex forms, making shading more structured and accurate. The body consists of **hard planes** (bony structures) and **soft planes** (muscles, fat, and skin). Each plane interacts with light differently, influencing the values in a drawing.

---

## **1. Major Planes of the Human Body**

The body can be broken down into **broad geometric planes** to simplify shading.

### **A. Head & Face Planes**

The head is often simplified using a **Loomis Head** or an **Asaro Planes Model** (a sculptural breakdown of facial planes).

- **Front Plane (Forehead, Nose Bridge, Cheekbones, Chin)** → Usually the **brightest** due to direct light exposure.
- **Side Planes (Temples, Side of Nose, Jawline, Side of Chin)** → Typically in **midtone** values.
- **Recessed Planes (Eye Sockets, Under the Nose, Lips, Neck Under Chin)** → Fall into **shadows**.
- **Transition Planes (Cheeks, Brow Ridge, Jaw Curve)** → Gradual light changes based on muscle structure.

### **B. Torso Planes**

The torso consists of **hard planes (bony areas)** and **soft planes (muscle & skin).**

- **Chest & Ribcage**
    
    - Upper chest (clavicle area) → **Lit plane** when facing light.
    - Ribcage sides & lower abs → **Midtones**.
    - Under the pecs & below the ribcage → **Shadow plane**.
- **Back & Shoulder Planes**
    
    - Shoulder blades create shifting planes that catch light differently.
    - Spine and trapezius muscles create a **central ridge**, dividing light and shadow.

### **C. Arms & Legs Planes**

Arms and legs have **cylindrical forms**, but their planes can be simplified into **box-like** structures for clarity.

- **Top Plane (Facing Light)** → Highlighted area.
- **Side Planes** → Midtone areas, depending on light angle.
- **Bottom Plane (Facing Away from Light)** → Shadowed area.
- **Knees & Elbows** – Hard bony planes catch highlights, while recessed areas (inner elbow, back of knee) fall into shadow.

### **D. Hands & Feet Planes**

- Hands are full of small, angular planes—knuckles and finger bones create strong shadow/light shifts.
- Feet, like hands, have **top-lit planes (highlights)** and **undersides (shadows)**.

## **3. Practical Application in Drawing**

- **Block Out Forms First** – Sketch major body parts as geometric shapes.
- **Identify Light & Shadow Groups** – Group large light and dark areas before refining.
- **Use Hard & Soft Edges** – Hard edges where planes shift abruptly (cheekbones, knees), soft edges for smooth transitions (biceps, stomach).
- **Reflected Light** – Don’t let shadows go too dark; bounce light adds realism.
