### **Form and Perspective in Drawing**

Both **form** and **perspective** are crucial for making drawings look three-dimensional and realistic.

---

## **1. Understanding Form**

**Form** refers to the three-dimensional structure of an object. While shapes are **flat (2D)**, forms have **volume (3D)** with depth, width, and height.

### **Basic Forms**

- **Sphere** (like a ball)
- **Cube** (like a box)
- **Cylinder** (like a can)
- **Cone** (like an ice cream cone)

### **How to Show Form in Drawing**

To make forms look three-dimensional, use:

1. **Values & Shading** – Light and shadow define the illusion of depth.
2. **Contour Lines** – Curved or angled lines help describe how a form turns in space.
3. **Foreshortening** – When an object appears shorter due to perspective, like an arm reaching toward the viewer.

## **2. Understanding Perspective**

**Perspective** creates the illusion of depth, making objects look realistic.

### **Types of Perspective**

#### **1-Point Perspective**

- Uses **one vanishing point** (VP) on the horizon line.
- Example: Looking down a straight road or hallway.

#### **2-Point Perspective**

- Uses **two vanishing points** (VPs) on the horizon.
- Example: A cube seen from the corner.

#### **3-Point Perspective**

- Uses **three vanishing points** (VPs), one for height.
- Example: Looking up at a tall skyscraper.

#### **Atmospheric Perspective**

- Distant objects appear **lighter and less detailed** (used in landscapes).
