# Form Drawing Exercises

## Drawing Basic Forms from Different Angles

- Draw cubes, spheres, cylinders, and cones from various viewpoints.
- Rotate them mentally and sketch how they would look.
- Focus on perspective and how light affects them.

## Cross-Contour Drawing

- Instead of just outlines, draw curved or angled lines wrapping around forms.
- Example: Draw a sphere, then add curved lines wrapping around it to show volume.
- Helps you understand how light moves over a surface.

## Form Combination

- Combine basic forms (e.g., a cylinder on top of a cube) to create complex objects.
- Helps when drawing characters, vehicles, or architecture.

# Perspective Drawing Exercises

## One-Point Perspective Box Grid

- Draw a horizon line and a **single vanishing point (VP)**.
- Sketch a grid of boxes receding toward the VP.
- Helps with understanding depth and consistent spacing.

## Two-Point Perspective Practice

- Draw a horizon line with **two vanishing points**.
- Sketch cubes or buildings using both VPs.
- Great for cityscapes and objects at an angle.

## Three-Point Perspective Drill

- Set up three vanishing points: two on the horizon, one above or below.
- Draw a tall building seen from **above or below**.
- Helps create dramatic perspectives.

## Foreshortening Practice

- Take a simple object (like a cylinder) and tilt it toward the viewer.
- Try drawing it from different angles to see how it changes shape.
- Helps when drawing people in dynamic poses.

## Atmospheric Perspective Study

- Find a landscape reference.
- Make objects in the foreground **darker and more detailed** while those in the background **fade and lose detail**.
- Helps create depth in scenery.
