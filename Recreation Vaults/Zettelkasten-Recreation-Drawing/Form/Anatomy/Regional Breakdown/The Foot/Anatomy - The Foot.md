The foot is a complex structure designed for locomotion, balance, and absorbing shock. Its major sections are the **ankle** (tarsus), the **midfoot** (metatarsus), and the **forefoot/toes** (phalanges).

# 1. Skeletal Structure
This is the foundational framework. The goal is to understand the arched structure that supports the body's weight.

- **Bones:** List the primary bone groups from proximal to distal.

    - [[Metatarsals]] (5 bones forming the main body/arch of the foot)

# 2. Bony Landmarks
These are crucial reference points for constructing the foot's complex, arched form.

- **Medial Malleolus (Tibia):** The prominent bony bump on the *inside* of the ankle.
- **Lateral Malleolus (Fibula):** The prominent bony bump on the *outside* of the ankle (sits lower and further back than the medial one).
- **Calcaneus (Heel Bone):** The large bone forming the back of the heel.
- **Base of 5th Metatarsal:** The bony bump on the *outer edge* of the foot, about halfway down.
- **Heads of Metatarsals (Ball of the Foot):** The "knuckles" of the foot where the toes begin.

# 3. Major Muscle Groups
Focus on the *intrinsic* muscles (within the foot) and the *extrinsic* tendons (from the leg) that define the surface form.

## Dorsum (Top of Foot)
- *[[Extrinsic Tendons]]:** The tendons from the shin muscles (like the Tibialis Anterior) are highly visible as they run down to the toes.
- *[[Extensor Digitorum Brevis]]:** A small, thin muscle on the outer side of the foot's top plane, creating a slight fleshy form.

## Plantar (Sole of Foot)
- *[[Plantar Fascia]]:** A thick, fibrous sheet (not a muscle) that runs from the heel to the toes, maintaining the longitudinal arch.
- *[[Abductor Hallucis / Abductor Digiti Minimi]]:** Fleshy muscle pads on the inner (big toe) and outer (pinky toe) edges of the sole.
- *[[Flexor Digitorum Brevis]]:** The central muscle pad on the sole, under the plantar fascia.

# 4. Articulation and Range of Motion
How the foot moves. This connects structure to gesture.

- **Ankle Joint (Talocrural):** A hinge joint (Tibia/Fibula/Talus). Allows **dorsiflexion** (foot up) and **plantarflexion** (foot down/pointing).
- **Subtalar Joint:** (Below the ankle) Allows **inversion** (sole in) and **eversion** (sole out).
- **Metatarsophalangeal (MTP) Joints (Ball of Foot):** Allow flexion/extension (curling/uncurling toes) and slight spreading.

# 5. Simplified Forms for Drawing
This section translates complex anatomy into simple, geometric shapes.

- **Main Form:** A **wedge** or a **tilted block**, triangular from the side (heel to toes) and arched on top.
- **Ankle:** A smaller block or "wrench" shape (the malleoli) gripping the main foot wedge.
- **Toes:** A group of small, hinged cylinders that follow the "ball of the foot" curve. The big toe is a larger, separate form.
- **Arch:** The foot is not flat. Think of it as a tripod (heel, 1st metatarsal head, 5th metatarsal head) with a domed arch (longitudinal and transverse).
