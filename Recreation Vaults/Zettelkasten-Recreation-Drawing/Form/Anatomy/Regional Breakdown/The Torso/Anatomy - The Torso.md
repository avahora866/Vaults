The front torso houses and protects vital organs while providing the core strength for spinal flexion and rotation. It is composed of the **thoracic region** (chest) and the **abdominal region** (stomach and pelvis).

# 1. Skeletal Structure
This is the foundational framework. The goal is to understand the rigid and semi-rigid structures that create the body's main mass.

# 2. Bony Landmarks
These are crucial reference points for measurement, placing muscles, and finding proportions.

- ***Suprasternal Notch (Pit of the Neck):*** The 'U' shape at the top of the sternum, between the clavicles.
- ***Clavicles:*** The S-shaped bones that define the top of the chest.
- ***Sternum:*** The flat, vertical "necktie" bone in the center of the chest.
- ***Costal Cartilage Arch:*** The V-shaped lower edge of the ribcage.
- ***Anterior Superior Iliac Spine (ASIS):*** The two prominent "hip points" at the front of the pelvis.
- ***Pubic Symphysis:*** The center point at the bottom of the pelvis.

# 3. Major Muscle Groups
Focus on the muscles that define the surface form.

## Thoracic Region (Chest)
- ***[[Pectoralis Major]]:** The large, fan-shaped muscle of the chest. It defines the "slab" of the chest and armpit.
- ***[[Serratus Anterior]]:** The "finger" or "saw" muscles on the side of the ribcage, under the armpit. They interlock with the obliques.

## Abdominal Region
- ***[[Rectus Abdominis]]:** The "six-pack" muscles. A long, flat sheet running vertically from the lower ribs to the pubic bone, divided by tendons.
- ***[[External Obliques]]:** The "love handles." A sheet of muscle on the sides of the abdomen, running from the ribs down to the iliac crest (pelvis).

# 4. Articulation and Range of Motion
How the torso moves. This connects structure to gesture.

- ***Thoracic & Lumbar Spine (Flexion):*** Allows the torso to bend forward (a "crunch" motion), compressing the abdominal muscles.
- ***Thoracic & Lumbar Spine (Lateral Flexion):*** Allows the torso to bend side-to-side, engaging the obliques.
- ***Thoracic & Lumbar Spine (Rotation):*** Allows the torso to twist.

# 5. Simplified Forms for Drawing
This section translates complex anatomy into simple, geometric shapes.

- ***Ribcage:*** An **egg** or **barrel** shape, wider at the top and back, and tapered toward the bottom. It is a rigid mass.
- ***Pelvis:*** A **bucket** or **tilted box**. This is also a rigid mass.
- ***Abdomen:*** The flexible, muscular "cylinder" that connects the two rigid masses of the ribcage and pelvis. The gesture of the torso is defined by the relationship (tilt and twist) between the ribcage and pelvis.
