The head houses the brain and primary sensory organs. Its main sections are the **cranium** (braincase) and the **facial structure** (jaws, nose, and eye sockets).

# 1. Skeletal Structure
This is the foundational framework. The goal is to understand the rigid structure that dictates the head's proportion and planes.

- ***Bones:*** List the primary bones or bone masses.
    - *[[Cranium]]* (A single, egg-like mass including the frontal, parietal, and occipital bones)
    - *[[Mandible]]* (The jaw, the only major mobile bone)
    - *[[Maxilla]]* (The upper jaw, which includes the nasal cavity)
    - *[[Zygomatic Arch]]* (The cheekbone, which connects the cranium to the face)

# 2. Bony Landmarks
These are crucial reference points for constructing the features and planes of the head.

- ***Brow Ridge (Superciliary Arch):*** The bony ridge above the eye sockets.
- ***Zygomatic Arch:*** The prominent cheekbone that extends from under the eye back toward the ear.
- ***Glabella:*** The smooth area between the brow ridges.
- ***Nasal Bone:*** The hard bridge of the nose.
- ***Mandible:*** The angle of the jaw (gonial angle) and the point of the chin (mental protuberance) are key points.
- ***Mastoid Process:*** The bony bump just behind the earlobe, where the SCM muscle of the neck attaches.

# 3. Major Muscle Groups
Focus on the muscles that define surface form (chewing) and create expression.

## Muscles of Mastication (Chewing)
*These create major forms on the side of the head.*
- ***[[Temporalis]]:** A large, fan-shaped muscle on the side of the head (the temple).
- ***[[Masseter]]:** A powerful, boxy muscle covering the angle of the jaw.

## Muscles of Facial Expression
*These are generally thin sheets of muscle that create wrinkles and move the features.*
- ***[[Frontalis]]:** The forehead muscle; raises the eyebrows.
- ***[[Orbicularis Oculi]]:** The sphincter muscle that closes the eye (causes crow's feet).
- ***[[Orbicularis Oris]]:** The sphincter muscle that closes the mouth.
- ***[[Zygomaticus Major]]:** Pulls the corners of the mouth up and back (smiling).

# 4. Articulation and Range of Motion
How the head moves. This connects structure to gesture.

- ***Temporomandibular Joint (TMJ):*** A hinge and gliding joint connecting the mandible to the cranium, just in front of the ear. Allows the jaw to open, close, and move side-to-side.
- ***Atlanto-occipital Joint (Skull/Neck):*** Connects the base of the skull (occipital bone) to the first vertebra (the atlas). Allows the "yes" nodding motion.

# 5. Simplified Forms for Drawing
This section translates complex anatomy into simple, geometric shapes.

- ***Cranium:*** A large sphere or ball.
- ***Facial Mass:*** A wedge or block attached to the front of the sphere, with the sides of the sphere often "sliced off" (Loomis method).
- ***Jaw (Mandible):*** A separate, hinged box or 'L' shape attached to the bottom of the facial mass.
- ***Muzzle:*** The area of the mouth and nose can be simplified as a cylinder or barrel shape.
