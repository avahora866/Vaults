The neck is a flexible column that supports and moves the head, connecting it to the torso. Its primary sections are the **posterior** (back) mass, defined by the trapezius, and the **anterior** (front) structures, defined by the SCMs and the vertical column of the larynx and trachea.

# 1. Skeletal Structure
This is the foundational framework. The goal is to understand the flexible column that provides support and movement.

- ***Bones:***
    - [[Cervical Vertebrae]]* (C1-C7): The stack of bones that form the flexible spinal column of the neck.
    - [[Hyoid Bone]]: A U-shaped bone in the front, which anchors the tongue and muscles of the lower mouth and larynx. It does not articulate with any other bone.

# 2. Bony Landmarks
These are crucial reference points for placing the major muscles and central features of the neck.

- ***Vertebra Prominens (C7):*** The prominent bump at the base of the back of the neck, where the neck joins the torso.
- ***Mastoid Process:*** The bony bump on the skull, located just behind the earlobe. The insertion point for the SCM.
- ***Clavicles & Manubrium of Sternum:*** (Part of the torso) These form the "pit of the neck" (suprasternal notch) and are the *origin* points for the SCM.
- ***Thyroid Cartilage ("Adam's Apple"):*** The hard, shield-like cartilage on the front of the neck. It is a key landmark for the front plane.

# 3. Major Muscle Groups
Focus on the muscles that define the surface form.

## Anterior/Lateral (Front and Side)
- **[[Sternocleidomastoid (SCM)]]:** The most important muscle for artists. A long, strap-like muscle that forms a prominent 'V' shape.
    - **Origin:** Sternum and Clavicle.
    - **Insertion:** Mastoid process (behind the ear).
- ***Strap Muscles (Infrahyoid/Suprahyoid groups):*** A group of small, vertical muscles on the front of the neck that flank the larynx and trachea. They create the cylindrical form between the SCMs.

## Posterior (Back)
- **[[Trapezius]] (Upper Fibers):** This is the large, diamond-shaped muscle of the upper back. Its upper fibers form the sloping silhouette from the skull down to the shoulders.
- **[[Scalenes]] & [[Levator Scapulae]]:** Deeper muscles on the side of the neck that add volume between the SCM and the Trapezius, forming the "side plane" of the neck.

# 4. Articulation and Range of Motion
How the head moves on the neck.

- ***Atlanto-Occipital Joint (Skull/C1):*** Allows for flexion and extension (the "yes" nodding motion).
- ***Atlanto-Axial Joint (C1/C2):*** Allows for rotation (the "no" shaking motion).
- ***Lower Cervical Vertebrae (C3-C7):*** Allow for combined movements of flexion, extension, lateral bending (tilting), and rotation.

# 5. Simplified Forms for Drawing
This section translates complex anatomy into simple, geometric shapes.

- ***Main Form:*** A simple **cylinder**.
- ***Angle:*** This cylinder is **not vertical**; it angles *forward* from its base at C7 to where it connects to the skull.
- ***Connections:*** Imagine the cylinder "plugging into" the torso at the pit of the neck and the C7, and into the skull behind the jaw.
- ***Key Muscles:*** The SCMs are two straps that wrap around this cylinder from behind the ears to the front base.
