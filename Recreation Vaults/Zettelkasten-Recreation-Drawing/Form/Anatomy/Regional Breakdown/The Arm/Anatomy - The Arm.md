The arm is the upper limb, used for manipulation, gesturing, and interaction. Its major sections are the **shoulder**, the **upper arm** (brachium), the **forearm** (antebrachium), and the **hand**.

# 1. Skeletal Structure
This is the foundational framework. The goal is to understand the rigid structure that dictates length and proportion.

- ***Bones:*** List the primary bones from proximal (closest to the torso) to distal (furthest from the torso).
    - [[Humerus]] (The single bone of the upper arm)
    - [[Radius]] (The forearm bone on the thumb side; it rotates)
    - [[Ulna]] (The forearm bone on the pinky side; it is stable and forms the elbow hinge)
    - *([[Carpals, Metacarpals, Phalanges]] form the hand, a separate study)*

# 2. Bony Landmarks
These are crucial reference points for measurement and construction because they are close to the skin's surface and are not obscured by muscle.

- ***Shoulder:***
    - ***Acromion Process:*** The bony tip of the shoulder.
    - ***Clavicle (Collarbone):** The S-shaped bone at the top of the chest, connecting to the acromion.
- ***Elbow:***
    - ***Olecranon Process of Ulna:*** The sharp "point" of the elbow, highly visible when the arm is bent.
    - ***Medial and Lateral Epicondyles of Humerus:*** The two bony bumps on either side of the elbow. The medial (inner) one is more prominent.
- ***Wrist:***
    - ***Styloid Process of Ulna:** The prominent bump on the pinky side of the wrist.
    - ***Styloid Process of Radius:** The bump on the thumb side of the wrist (less prominent).

# 3. Major Muscle Groups
Focus on the muscles that define the surface form. Organize them by region and function.

## Shoulder
- **[[Deltoid]]:** The "shoulder cap." A thick, triangular muscle that gives the shoulder its round form and lifts the arm.

## Upper Arm
- ***Anterior (Flexors):***
    - [[Biceps Brachii]]: Creates the main bulge on the front of the upper arm. Flexes the elbow and supinates (rotates palm up) the forearm.
- ***Posterior (Extensors):***
    - [[Triceps Brachii]]: Defines the back of the upper arm; has three heads. Straightens the elbow.*

## Forearm
*The forearm is complex. Start by grouping the muscles.*
- ***Extensor Group (Top/Posterior):** Pulls the hand and fingers back (extension). Originates from the lateral epicondyle.
- ***Flexor Group (Underside/Anterior):** Curls the hand and fingers (flexion). Originates from the medial epicondyle.
- ***Brachioradialis:** A key muscle that wraps from the outer elbow (humerus) down towards the thumb side of the wrist. Defines the top plane of the forearm.*

# 4. Articulation and Range of Motion
How the limb moves. This connects structure to gesture.

- ***Shoulder (Ball-and-socket):** Allows for a wide range of motion (flexion, extension, rotation).
- ***Elbow (Hinge):** Limited to flexion and extension (bending and straightening).
- ***Forearm (Pivot):** The Radius rotates around the Ulna, allowing **pronation** (palm down) and **supination** (palm up). This is key to drawing the arm.
- ***Wrist (Condyloid):** Allows for up/down and side-to-side movement.

# 5. Simplified Forms for Drawing
This section translates complex anatomy into simple, geometric shapes that are easier to draw in perspective.

- **Upper Arm:** A simple cylinder.
- **Forearm:** A tapered cylinder or a flattened box, wider near the elbow (muscle mass) and narrower at the wrist (tendons).
- **Joints:** Think of the shoulder, elbow, and wrist as simple spheres or hinges connecting the main forms.
