The pelvis is a basin-shaped ring of bone that connects the spinal column to the legs. It supports the weight of the upper body, protects internal organs, and serves as the anchor for the powerful muscles of the legs and lower torso.

# 1. Skeletal Structure
This is the foundational framework. The goal is to understand the single, rigid mass that forms the base of the torso and connects to the legs.

- ***Bones:***
    - *[[Ilium]]* (The large, wing-like superior part)
    - *[[Ischium]]* (The lower, posterior part, or "sitting bones")
    - *[[Pubis]]* (The anterior, inferior part that meets in the middle)
    - *[[Sacrum]]* (The triangular bone at the base of the spine, fused to the ilia)
    - *[[Coccyx]]* (The tailbone)

# 2. Bony Landmarks
These are crucial reference points for finding the base of the torso and the top of the legs.

- ***Anterior (Front):***
    - ***Iliac Crest:** The top, curved edge of the hip bone; you can feel this at your waist.
    - ***Anterior Superior Iliac Spine (ASIS):** The "hip points" at the front end of the iliac crest. A key landmark for proportion.
    - ***Pubic Symphysis:** The fixed joint at the center-front of the pelvis.
- ***Posterior (Back):***
    - ***Posterior Superior Iliac Spine (PSIS):** The two "dimples" on the lower back, where the sacrum joins the ilia.
    - ***Sacrum:** The flat, triangular bone surface between the PSIS dimples.
    - ***Ischial Tuberosity:** The "sitting bones." The lowest point of the pelvis; the hamstrings attach here.

# 3. Major Muscle Groups
Focus on the muscles that originate from the pelvis and define the form of the hips and buttocks.

## Posterior/Lateral (Buttocks and Side)
- ***[[Gluteus Maximus]]:** The largest, most superficial muscle of the buttocks. It creates the main rounded form.
- ***[[Gluteus Medius]]:** Located on the side of the hip, above and partially under the maximus. It defines the side-plane of the hip.
- ***[[Tensor Fasciae Latae (TFL)]]:** A small muscle at the front/side of the hip (near the ASIS) that transitions into the IT band. It creates a small, distinct form.

## Anterior (Hip Flexors)
- ***[[Iliopsoas]]:** (Deep) A primary hip flexor that connects the lumbar spine and ilium to the femur. It is not visible but its mass contributes to the form.
- ***Adductor Group:** (Inner Thigh) A group of muscles originating from the pubic bone and running down the inner thigh.

# 4. Articulation and Range of Motion
How the pelvis connects the spine and legs.

- ***Hip Joint (Acetabulum):** A deep ball-and-socket joint where the head of the femur (leg) connects to the pelvis. Allows a wide range of motion (flexion, extension, rotation, abduction/adduction).
- ***Sacroiliac (SI) Joint:** The rigid joint connecting the sacrum to the ilium. Has very little movement.
- ***Lumbar Spine:** The pelvis tilts forward (anterior tilt) and backward (posterior tilt) in relation to the lumbar spine, which is key for gesture and posture.

# 5. Simplified Forms for Drawing
This section translates complex anatomy into simple, geometric shapes.

- ***Main Form:*** A **bucket**, a **tilted box**, or a **basin**.
- ***Angle:*** The pelvis is **tilted forward** (anterior tilt) in a standard standing pose. The ASIS points are lower than the PSIS points.
- ***Construction:*** Often drawn as a "butterfly" or "moth" shape from the front, and a simple wedge or box from the side. It is a single, rigid mass.
