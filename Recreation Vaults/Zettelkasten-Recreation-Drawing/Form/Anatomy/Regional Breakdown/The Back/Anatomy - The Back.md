The back provides core support for posture and is the origin for powerful pulling motions of the arms. It is composed of the **upper back** (shoulders and trapezius), the **middle back** (lats and scapulae), and the **lower back** (lumbar spine and pelvis).

# 2. Bony Landmarks
These are crucial reference points for finding the position of the spine and scapulae.

- ***Vertebra Prominens (C7):*** The prominent bump at the base of the neck.
- ***Spinous Processes of Vertebrae:*** The series of small bumps that form the "ridge" of the spine, often visible as a furrow.
- ***Spine of the Scapula:*** The horizontal ridge on the shoulder blade that ends at the Acromion.
- ***Medial Border of the Scapula:*** The inner edge of the shoulder blade, which becomes very clear when the arm is pulled forward.
- ***Inferior Angle of the Scapula:*** The bottom tip of the shoulder blade.
- ***Posterior Superior Iliac Spine (PSIS):*** The two "dimples" at the base of the lower back, where the pelvis joins the sacrum.
- ***Iliac Crest (Posterior):*** The top edge of the hip bones, a key landmark for the base of the torso.

# 3. Major Muscle Groups
Focus on the large, superficial muscles that define the "V-shape" of the back and the forms of the shoulder blades.

## Upper Back
- ***[[Trapezius]]:** A large, diamond-shaped muscle covering the upper back. It has three sections:
    - **Upper Fibers:** Shrug the shoulders.
    - **Middle Fibers:** Pull the shoulder blades together (retraction).
    - **Lower Fibers:** Pull the shoulder blades down.
- ***[[Deltoid]] (Posterior Head):** The rear part of the shoulder cap, crucial for the back's silhouette.

## Middle and Side Back
- ***[[Latissimus Dorsi]] ("Lats"):** The largest muscle of the back. A fan-shaped muscle creating the "V-taper" of the torso. It originates from the lower spine/pelvis and inserts into the upper arm (humerus).
- ***[[Teres Major]]:** A smaller muscle that works with the Lats, connecting the scapula to the humerus.
- ***Infraspinatus & Teres Minor:*** (Part of the rotator cuff) These muscles cover the body of the scapula, creating its fleshy form.

## Lower Back
- ***[[Erector Spinae]]:** Two thick columns of muscle that run vertically on either side of the lumbar spine, filling the "spinal furrow."

# 4. Articulation and Range of Motion
How the back moves. This connects structure to gesture.

- ***Scapular Movement:*** The scapulae are not fixed. They can **elevate/depress** (shrugging), **retract** (squeezing together), **protract** (rounding forward), and **rotate** (when raising the arm overhead).
- ***Spinal Extension:*** Bending backward, which engages the Erector Spinae.
- ***Spinal Flexion:*** Bending forward, which stretches the back muscles.
- ***Spinal Rotation/Lateral Flexion:*** Twisting and bending side-to-side.

# 5. Simplified Forms for Drawing
This section translates complex anatomy into simple, geometric shapes.

- ***Upper Back:*** A large, somewhat flat "shield" or wedge shape formed by the Trapezius and Deltoids.
- ***Scapulae:*** Two flat, triangular "plates" that glide over the ribcage. Their position is critical and changes with every arm movement.
- ***Lats:*** Two large "wings" or fan shapes that connect the arms to the lower back, defining the torso's V-shape.
- ***Erector Spinae:*** Two simple "logs" or cylinders on either side of the lower spine.
