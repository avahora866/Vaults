The hand is the complex terminal part of the arm, designed for fine manipulation, gripping, and sensation. Its major sections are the **wrist** (carpus), the **palm** (metacarpus), and the **fingers** (phalanges).

# 1. Skeletal Structure
This is the foundational framework. The goal is to understand the complex, flexible structure that allows for dexterity.

# 2. Bony Landmarks
These are crucial reference points for constructing the hand and placing the fingers.

- ***Knuckles (Metacarpophalangeal Joints):** The prominent bumps where the fingers join the palm.
- ***Interphalangeal Joints:** The smaller knuckles in the middle and at the tips of the fingers.
- ***Styloid Process of Ulna/Radius:** (Part of the forearm) These define the width and articulation of the wrist.
- ***Pisiform Bone:** A small, pea-shaped carpal bone that creates a bump at the base of the palm on the pinky side.

# 3. Major Muscle Groups
Focus on the muscles *within the hand* that define the surface form. (Note: Most finger movement is controlled by *extrinsic* muscles in the forearm, whose tendons run into the hand).

## Palm (Intrinsic Muscles)
- **[[Thenar Eminence]]:** The large, fleshy muscle mass at the base of the thumb. This is crucial for thumb opposition and grip.
- **[[Hypothenar Eminence]]:** The smaller, fleshy muscle mass on the pinky side of the palm.
- ***Interossei & Lumbricals:** (Deep) These small muscles are located *between* the metacarpals, giving the back of the hand a slightly padded or webbed appearance between the knuckles.

# 4. Articulation and Range of Motion
How the hand moves. This connects structure to gesture.

- ***Wrist (Radiocarpal/Midcarpal):** A condyloid and gliding joint system. Allows flexion (bending down), extension (bending back), and side-to-side deviation.
- ***Metacarpophalangeal (MCP) Joints (Knuckles):** Condyloid joints. Allow flexion/extension (making a fist) and side-to-side spreading of the fingers.
- ***Interphalangeal (IP) Joints (Finger Joints):** Hinge joints. Limited to flexion and extension.
- ***Thumb (Carpometacarpal Joint):** A saddle joint. This is the most important joint, allowing **opposition** (touching the thumb to the other fingertips).

# 5. Simplified Forms for Drawing
This section translates complex anatomy into simple, geometric shapes.

- ***Palm:*** A flattened **box** or a simple, stiff **mitten shape**. The back of the hand is flatter; the palm side is more concave.
- ***Fingers:*** A series of **cylinders** connected by hinge joints. They taper slightly toward the tips.
- ***Thumb:*** A smaller, two-jointed cylinder attached to a **triangular wedge** (the thenar eminence) that plugs into the side of the main palm box.
