The leg is the lower limb, designed for locomotion and supporting the body's weight. Its major sections are the **thigh** (upper leg), the **lower leg** (crus), and the **foot**.

# 1. Skeletal Structure
This is the foundational framework. The goal is to understand the rigid structure that dictates length and proportion.

- **Bones:** List the primary bones from proximal (closest to the torso) to distal (furthest from the torso).
    - [[Femur]] (The single, large bone of the thigh)
    - [[Patella]] (The "kneecap," a sesamoid bone)
    - [[Tibia]] (The large, weight-bearing shin bone of the lower leg)
    - [[Fibula]] (The slender, non-weight-bearing bone on the outside of the lower leg)
    - ([[Tarsals, Metatarsals, Phalanges]] form the foot, a separate study)

# 2. Bony Landmarks
These are crucial reference points for measurement and construction because they are close to the skin's surface.

- **Hip:**
    - **Greater Trochanter:** The wide, bony knob on the *outside* of the femur, just below the hip. This is often the widest point of the hips.
- **Knee:**
    - **Patella:** The "kneecap" itself.
    - **Medial and Lateral Condyles of Femur/Tibia:** The wide, bony bumps on either side of the knee joint.
    - **Tibial Tuberosity:** The bony bump on the front of the shin, just below the patella, where the quadriceps tendon attaches.
- **Ankle:**
    - **Medial Malleolus:** The prominent bony bump on the *inside* of the ankle (part of the Tibia).
    - **Lateral Malleolus:** The prominent bony bump on the *outside* of the ankle (part of the Fibula). It is positioned *lower* and *further back* than the medial malleolus.

# 3. Major Muscle Groups
Focus on the muscles that define the surface form. Organize them by region and function.

## Thigh (Upper Leg)
- **Anterior (Extensors):**
    - [[Quadriceps Femoris]]: The "Quads." A large group of four muscles that define the front of the thigh. They straighten the knee.*
- **Posterior (Flexors):**
    - [[Hamstrings]]: A group of three muscles that define the back of the thigh. They bend the knee.*
- **Medial (Adductors):**
    - [[Adductor Group]]: A fan-shaped group of muscles on the inside of the thigh that pulls the leg inward.*

## Lower Leg
- **Posterior (Plantarflexors):**
    - [[Gastrocnemius]]: The "Calf." The diamond-shaped, two-headed muscle on the back of the lower leg. It points the foot down.*
    - [[Soleus]]: A flatter muscle located *underneath* the Gastrocnemius, visible on the sides of the lower leg.*
- **Anterior (Dorsiflexors):**
    - [[Tibialis Anterior]]: The "Shin" muscle. It runs alongside the tibia and pulls the foot upward.*
- **Connecting Tendon:**
    - [[Achilles Tendon]]: The large, strong tendon connecting the calf muscles (Gastrocnemius and Soleus) to the heel bone.*

# 4. Articulation and Range of Motion
How the limb moves. This connects structure to gesture.

- **Hip (Ball-and-socket):** Allows for a wide range of motion (flexion, extension, rotation, abduction/adduction).*
- **Knee (Hinge):** Limited to flexion and extension (bending and straightening).*
- **Ankle (Hinge):** Allows for **dorsiflexion** (foot up) and **plantarflexion** (foot down).*

# 5. Simplified Forms for Drawing
This section translates complex anatomy into simple, geometric shapes that are easier to draw in perspective.

- **Thigh:** A large, tapered cylinder, wider at the top and narrower at the knee.*
- **Lower Leg:** A tapered cylinder, wider at the top (calf muscle) and much narrower at the ankle. Note the S-curve of the tibia (shin bone) on the front plane.
- **Joints:** Think of the hip as a ball joint, and the knee and ankle as simple hinges connecting the main forms.*
