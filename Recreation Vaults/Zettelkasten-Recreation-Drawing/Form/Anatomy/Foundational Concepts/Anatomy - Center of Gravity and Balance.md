It's the hypothetical point where the entire weight of the body is considered to be concentrated.
The center of mass is the point where the mass of the body is evenly distributed, while the center of gravity is the point where the force of gravity is considered to act on the body. For practical purposes in relation to human movement, they can be thought of as the same point.

### **Location of the Center of Gravity** 
In a person standing in the anatomical position (upright, with arms at the sides), the center of gravity is generally located in the pelvic region, just in front of the second sacral vertebra. However, it's important to understand that this is not a fixed point. The exact location of the center of gravity is dynamic and changes with every movement of the body. When you raise your arms, for example, your center of gravity moves upward.

### **Factors Affecting the Center of Gravity** 
Several factors can influence the position of your center of gravity:

- **Body Shape and Composition:** The distribution of muscle, fat, and bone in your body will affect your center of gravity. For instance, individuals with more muscle mass in their upper body will have a higher center of gravity.
- **Age:** The center of gravity is higher in children due to the relatively larger size of their heads compared to the rest of their bodies. As they grow and their body proportions change, their center of gravity lowers.
- **Gender:** On average, men have a slightly higher center of gravity than women. This is primarily due to differences in body composition and bone structure, with men typically having broader shoulders and more muscle mass in the upper body, while women tend to have a wider pelvis.
- **Position and Movement:** As mentioned earlier, any change in your body's position will cause a shift in your center of gravity. This is a fundamental principle used by athletes, dancers, and gymnasts to control their movements.
- **External Factors:** Carrying external objects, such as a backpack or a heavy suitcase, will also alter your center of gravity. The new center of gravity will be a composite of your body and the object you are carrying.

### **The Relationship Between Center of Gravity and Balance** 
Balance is the ability to maintain your center of gravity over your **base of support**. Your base of support is the area beneath you that is in contact with the ground. When you are standing, your base of support is the area between and under your feet.

To maintain balance, your center of gravity must remain within your base of support. If your center of gravity moves outside of your base of support, you will become unstable and may fall.

Here are some key principles that govern the relationship between the center of gravity and balance:

- **A lower center of gravity increases stability.** This is why a wider stance and a lower body position are often used in sports that require a high degree of stability, such as wrestling or martial arts.
- **A larger base of support increases stability.** Spreading your feet apart widens your base of support, making it more difficult for your center of gravity to move outside of it.
- **The alignment of the body is crucial for balance.** Proper posture and alignment help to keep the center of gravity within the base of support, reducing the strain on muscles and joints.

By understanding these principles, you can improve your balance and stability in a variety of activities. For example, when lifting a heavy object, you should bend your knees and keep your back straight to lower your center of gravity and maintain a stable base.
