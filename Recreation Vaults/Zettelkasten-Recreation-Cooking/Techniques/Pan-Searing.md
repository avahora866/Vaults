Pan-searing is a high-heat cooking technique used primarily for proteins (steaks, chops, fish) to create a crisp, browned crust while maintaining a juicy interior. It is the fastest way to build complex flavor on meat.

### 1. The Science of Pan-Searing
Searing is not just about "browning"; it is about a specific chemical reaction that occurs at high temperatures.

* **The Maillard Reaction:** This is the holy grail of searing. When amino acids (proteins) and reducing sugars hit temperatures above **$140^\circ\text{C}$ ($285^\circ\text{F}$)**, they rearrange into rings and collections of rings that reflect light in such a way that the food turns brown. More importantly, this creates hundreds of new distinct flavor compounds (savory, nutty, roasted notes).
* **Surface Dehydration:** Water boils at $100^\circ\text{C}$. As long as there is moisture on the surface of your meat, the temperature cannot rise above $100^\circ\text{C}$. Therefore, you **cannot** sear wet meat; you are technically steaming it. The energy of the pan must first evaporate all surface water before the temperature can climb to the $140^\circ\text{C}+$ needed for the Maillard reaction.
* **Thermal Mass:** To sear effectively, the pan must hold more heat energy than the cold steak absorbs. If you put a cold steak into a thin, light pan, the pan's temperature will plummet instantly, killing the sear. A heavy pan (high thermal mass) acts as a heat "battery," keeping the temperature stable even when cold food is added.

### 2. Two Distinct Methods
While the searing action is the same, the workflow changes based on the thickness of the cut.

#### Method A: Sear-Roast (Thick Cuts & Poultry)
Best for thick steaks ($>1$ inch), bone-in chops, or chicken breasts.

1.  **Hard Sear:** Preheat pan with high-smoke-point oil until shimmering. Lay the meat away from you (to avoid splash). Sear undisturbed for 2–3 minutes until a deep brown crust forms. Flip.
2.  **Oven Finish:** Immediately transfer the entire pan into a preheated oven ($200^\circ\text{C}$/$400^\circ\text{F}$).
3.  **Why:** If you tried to cook a thick steak purely on the stove, the outside would burn before the center cooked. The oven provides gentle ambient heat to finish the center without burning the crust.

#### Method B: The "Butter Baste" Finish (Thinner Steaks/Fish)
Best for thinner steaks, scallops, or fish fillets.

1.  **Full Sear:** Cook the first side hard (about 70% of total cooking time).
2.  **Flip & Lower:** Flip the meat and immediately turn the heat down to medium/low.
3.  **The "Arroser" (Basting):** Add a generous knob of butter, crushed garlic, and herbs (thyme/rosemary) to the pan. Tilt the pan slightly so the melting butter pools at the bottom.
4.  **Spoon:** Use a large spoon to repeatedly scoop the foaming hot butter over the meat. This cooks the meat gently from the top while the bottom finishes, infusing it with aromatic fat.

### 3. Essential Variables

* **The Oil:** You must use an oil with a high **smoke point**.
    * *Good:* Avocado ($270^\circ\text{C}$), Grapeseed ($215^\circ\text{C}$), Canola, Clarified Butter (Ghee).
    * *Bad:* Butter (burns at $175^\circ\text{C}$), Extra Virgin Olive Oil (burns at $190^\circ\text{C}$ and turns bitter).
* **Dryness:** The meat must be patted bone-dry with paper towels before seasoning.
* **The "Release":** When protein is seared hard, it initially bonds to the metal of the pan. Do not force it. Once the crust is formed (carbonized), it will naturally release from the metal. If it sticks, it’s not ready.

### 4. Common Mistakes & Troubleshooting

* **The "Grey Ring":** A steak with a grey band of overcooked meat just under the crust. This happens when the steak was too cold (straight from the fridge) or the pan wasn't hot enough, requiring a longer cook time to get a crust. **Fix:** Let meat temper (sit at room temp) for 30-45 minutes before cooking.
* **Steaming not Searing:** The pan was overcrowded. If steaks are touching, moisture gets trapped between them, creating a steam bath. **Fix:** Leave at least 1 inch of space between items. Cook in batches if necessary.
* **Acrid/Burnt Flavor:** You used butter at the start of the sear. The milk solids in the butter burned black. **Fix:** Sear with oil first; add butter only in the last 2 minutes for flavor.

### 5. Tools
* **Cast Iron Skillet:** The gold standard. It has immense thermal mass and radiates heat evenly.
* **Stainless Steel Pan:** excellent for creating "fond" (the sticky brown bits left behind) which is crucial if you plan to make a pan sauce afterwards.
* **Splatter Guard:** A mesh screen to place over the pan, as proper searing will inevitably cause oil to spit.
