**Ingredient:** Vinegar
**(Optional) Scientific Name:** Acetic acid solution (*CH₃COOH* in *H₂O*)

# Summary
A sour, acidic liquid produced by the double fermentation of various sugary liquids (e.g., wine, cider, grain mash) into ethanol and then acetic acid, used primarily for flavor, preservation, and tenderizing.

# Flavor and Aroma
* **Flavor Profile:** Primarily **sour** and **acidic**. Secondary flavors depend on the source material (e.g., fruity from wine/cider, malty from barley, subtly sweet from rice). Can range from sharp and pungent to mellow and complex.
* **Aroma Profile:** Sharp, pungent, "vinegary." Specific aromas reflect the source (e.g., apples, grapes, malt).
* **Key Compounds:**
    * **Acetic Acid:** Responsible for the primary sour taste and pungent aroma.
    * **Esters, Aldehydes, Ketones:** Various volatile compounds derived from the source material and fermentation process contribute to the specific aroma profile (e.g., ethyl acetate).
    * **Other Acids:** Trace amounts of acids from the source material (e.g., tartaric acid in wine vinegar, malic acid in cider vinegar) add complexity.

# Culinary Function
* **Primary Role:** Provides **acidity**, enhances and balances flavors, acts as a **preservative** (pickling), and **tenderizes** (marinades).
* **Effect of Heat:** The pungent aroma intensifies when heated. Acidity remains, but prolonged cooking or reduction can mellow the sharpness and concentrate the source flavors. Used for **deglazing** pans.
* **Interactions:**
    * Cuts through richness and fat.
    * Brightens and sharpens flavors, preventing dishes from tasting flat.
    * Balances sweetness (e.g., in sweet and sour sauces, BBQ sauces, glazes).
    * Helps emulsify oil and water (vinaigrettes).
    * Denatures proteins, aiding in tenderizing marinades.
    * Reacts with baking soda for leavening.
    * Slows enzymatic browning in fruits and vegetables.

# Common Forms
* **White Distilled:** Made from distilled grain alcohol. Neutral flavor, high sharp acidity. Common for pickling and cleaning.
* **Apple Cider:** Made from fermented apple cider. Fruity, amber color, milder acidity. Good for dressings, marinades, chutneys. Often consumed for purported health benefits.
* **Wine (Red & White):** Made from respective wines. More complex, fruity flavors reflecting the wine. Used in vinaigrettes, sauces, deglazing.
* **Balsamic:** Made from reduced grape must (juice), aged in wooden barrels (especially Traditional Balsamic Vinegar from Modena/Reggio Emilia). Ranges from tangy (commercial) to complex, sweet, and syrupy (aged). Used in dressings, glazes, or drizzled as a finish.
* **Rice:** Made from fermented rice wine. Typically mild, clean, and subtly sweet. Essential in East Asian cuisine (sushi rice seasoning, dipping sauces, stir-fries).
* **Malt:** Made from malted barley mash (like beer). Robust, nutty, malty flavor. Classic condiment for fish and chips, used in pickling.
* **Sherry:** Made from sherry wine, aged. Complex, nutty, slightly sweet flavor. Used in Spanish cuisine, dressings, sauces.

# Preparation
* Usually used directly from the bottle.
* Can be **reduced** by simmering to create a thicker glaze and concentrate flavors (common with balsamic vinegar).
* Can be **infused** with herbs, spices, or fruits.

# Pairings and Cuisine
* **Classic Pairings:** **Olive oil** (vinaigrettes), salt, sugar/honey, mustard, herbs (dill, tarragon, oregano), garlic.
* **Protein Pairings:** Pork (with cider/balsamic), chicken (marinades), fish (malt/white wine), beef (red wine/balsamic).
* **Spice Pairings:** Pickling spices (mustard seed, peppercorns, bay leaf, dill seed), depends heavily on vinegar type and cuisine.
* **Common Cuisines:** Universal. Salad dressings, pickles, marinades, sauces (Hollandaise, Béarnaise, gastriques, sweet & sour, BBQ), deglazing, seasoning sushi rice, condiment (e.g., with fish and chips).

# Substitutions
* **For Acidity:** **Lemon juice** or **lime juice** (best substitutes, add citrus flavor). Use roughly 1:1, adjust to taste.
* **For Deglazing/Sauces:** Dry **white wine** (less acidic, adds alcohol flavor).
* **Milder Acidity:** **Verjus** (unripe grape juice).
* **Note:** Substitute based on the *function* needed (acidity, flavor). The specific flavor profile of the original vinegar will be lost. Avoid substituting strong vinegars like malt or balsamic unless the flavor profile is compatible.
