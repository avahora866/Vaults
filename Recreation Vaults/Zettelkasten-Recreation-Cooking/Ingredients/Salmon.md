**Ingredient:** Salmon
**(Optional) Scientific Name:** *Salmo salar* (Atlantic salmon), *Oncorhynchus* (Pacific salmon genus)

# Summary
A popular oily fish prized for its rich, high-fat flesh, distinct pink-orange color, and robust flavor.

# Flavor and Aroma
* **Flavor Profile:** Rich, oily, savory (umami), buttery, mildly "fishy" (in a pleasant, briny way). Flavor varies by species (e.g., King/Chinook is very rich, Sockeye is stronger, Coho is milder).
* **Aroma Profile:** Briny, oceanic, clean.
* **Key Compounds:** High in **Omega-3 fatty acids** (EPA and DHA), which contribute to its rich taste and mouthfeel. **Astaxanthin** (an antioxidant) provides the characteristic pink color but does not strongly influence flavor. Rich in **glutamates** (umami).

# Culinary Function
* **Primary Role:** Center-of-the-plate protein. Its high fat content makes it versatile and forgiving.
* **Effect of Heat:** Cooks quickly. The fat melts, basting the flesh and keeping it moist. The protein flakes easily when done. Skin becomes very crisp when seared or roasted at high heat. Overcooking makes it dry and chalky.
* **Interactions:** The rich fat stands up to strong, acidic, or herbaceous flavors. It cuts through creamy sauces and starches.

# Common Forms
* **Fresh/Frozen Fillets:** Most common. Sold skin-on or skinless, often pin-boned.
* **Fresh/Frozen Steaks:** Cross-section cuts, bone-in. Less common than fillets.
* **Smoked (Cold):** Cured and smoked at low temperatures. Silky, raw-like texture (lox). Used for bagels, canapés.
* **Smoked (Hot):** Cured and smoked at higher temperatures, cooking the fish. Flaky texture. Eaten as a main or in salads.
* **Canned:** Cooked and preserved in tins, often with skin and bones (which are edible and add calcium). Used for salads, burgers, or croquettes.
* **Cured (Gravlax):** Raw salmon cured in salt, sugar, and dill. Not smoked.

# Preparation
* **Check for Pin Bones:** Run fingers against the grain along the centerline of the fillet to find and remove small bones with pliers or tweezers.
* **Pat Dry:** Always pat the fish (especially the skin) completely dry with a paper towel before cooking to ensure a good sear.
* **Cooking (Skin-On):** Score the skin lightly. Cook skin-side down in a hot, oiled pan for ~80% of the total time to achieve crispy skin.
* **Cooking (Baking/Roasting):** Good for hands-off cooking. Often done at 200°C (400°F) for 12-15 minutes.
* **Raw:** High-quality, parasite-free (often previously frozen) salmon is used for sashimi, sushi, and tartare.

# Pairings and Cuisine
* **Classic Pairings:** **Lemon**, **dill**, asparagus, capers, cream cheese, mustard (especially Dijon).
* **Protein Pairings:** Often stands alone, but bacon (lardons) can be a pairing.
* **Spice/Herb Pairings:** Dill, chives, parsley, garlic, black pepper, fennel.
* **Common Cuisines:** Nordic (cured/smoked), Japanese (sushi/sashimi, teriyaki), Pacific Northwest (grilled, planked), European (baked, pan-seared).

# Substitutions
* **Best Match:** **Arctic Char** (very similar fat content, flavor, and texture) or **Steelhead Trout** (often mistaken for salmon).
* **In a Pinch (Different Profile):** Mackerel (stronger, oilier), Bluefish (stronger), large Rainbow Trout (milder, less fat).
