**Ingredient:** Rice
**(Optional) Scientific Name:** *Oryza sativa* (Asian rice)

# Summary
A staple cereal grain that serves as a primary carbohydrate, a neutral base for flavor, and a key textural component (fluffy, sticky, or creamy).

# Flavor and Aroma
* **Flavor Profile:** Generally neutral, starchy, and mildly sweet. Brown rice has a nuttier, earthier flavor.
* **Aroma Profile:** Varies significantly. Most are neutral when raw. Aromatic varieties are prized for their scent.
* **Key Compounds:**
    * **Starch:** The primary component, a blend of **amylose** (linear) and **amylopectin** (branched). The ratio of these two starches dictates the rice's cooked texture.
    * **2-acetyl-1-pyrroline (2AP):** The primary aromatic compound in Basmati and Jasmine rice, giving them their characteristic popcorn-like or floral aroma.

# Culinary Function
* **Primary Role:** To provide bulk, starch, and a neutral canvas for other ingredients. Its function is defined by its starch ratio.
* **Effect of Heat:** Starches gelatinize when cooked with water, causing the grains to swell and soften.
    * **High-Amylose (Long-Grain):** Grains remain firm, fluffy, and separate. Amylose is a straight-chain starch that doesn't "gel" as much.
    * **High-Amylopectin (Short/Medium-Grain):** Grains become moist, tender, and stick or clump together. Amylopectin is a branched starch that leaches out and creates a sticky or creamy sauce.
* **Interactions:** Readily absorbs the flavor of cooking liquids, such as stock, coconut milk, or aromatic-infused water.

# Common Forms
* **Long-Grain:** (High Amylose) Fluffy, separate grains.
    * *Examples:* **Basmati** (aromatic, nutty), **Jasmine** (aromatic, softer), standard American long-grain.
    * *Uses:* Pilafs, biryanis, fried rice, side dishes.
* **Medium-Grain:** (Balanced Amylose/Amylopectin) Moist, tender, creamy.
    * *Examples:* **Arborio**, **Carnaroli** (for risotto), **Bomba**, **Calasparra** (for paella).
    * *Uses:* Risotto, paella.
* **Short-Grain:** (High Amylopectin) Soft and sticky, clumps together.
    * *Examples:* **Japonica** (sushi rice), **Calrose**.
    * *Uses:* Sushi, onigiri, rice bowls.
* **Glutinous (Sweet) Rice:** Almost entirely amylopectin. Opaque, very sticky, and chewy when cooked.
    * *Uses:* Desserts (e.g., mango sticky rice), rice cakes, dumplings.
* **Whole-Grain (Brown) Rice:** The entire grain (bran, germ, endosperm). Chewier, nuttier, and takes longer to cook. Can be long- or short-grain.
* **Parboiled (Converted) Rice:** Steamed under pressure before milling. Grains are firmer, more separate, and retain more nutrients than standard white rice.

# Preparation
* **Rinsing:** Most white rice forms (except risotto rice) should be rinsed in cold water until the water runs clear. This removes excess surface starch and prevents a gummy, clumped texture.
* **Soaking:** Often used for Basmati to help the grains elongate and for brown rice to reduce cooking time.
* **Absorption Method:** The most common technique. Rice is cooked in a measured amount of liquid (e.g., 1 part rice to 1.5 parts water) in a covered pot until the liquid is fully absorbed.
* **Risotto Method:** Grains are *not* rinsed. They are first toasted in fat, then hot liquid is added in small increments, stirring constantly to release the amylopectin and create a creamy sauce.

# Pairings and Cuisine
* **Classic Pairings:** Legumes (beans, lentils), soy sauce, coconut milk, saffron, butter, garlic, ginger. It is a base for nearly everything.
* **Protein Pairings:** Chicken, beef, fish, tofu, eggs.
* **Spice Pairings:** Cumin, turmeric, cardamom, star anise, cilantro, parsley.
* **Common Cuisines:** Global staple.
    * *Asia:* Sushi, fried rice, congee, biryani, nasi lemak.
    * *Europe:* Risotto (Italy), Paella (Spain), rice pudding.
    * *Americas:* Arroz con pollo, jambalaya, rice and beans.

# Substitutions
* **Note:** Rice *types* are generally not interchangeable (e.g., Basmati cannot make risotto). Substitutions are for rice as a *category*.
* **Fluffy Grains:** Quinoa, bulgur wheat, farro, couscous.
* **Creamy Grains (for Risotto):** Pearled barley, farro (farrotto).
* **Low-Carb:** Riced cauliflower, riced broccoli.
