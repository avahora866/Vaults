**Ingredient:** Egg (Chicken)
**(Optional) Scientific Name:** *Gallus gallus domesticus* (species) - The egg itself doesn't have a distinct scientific name.

# Summary
An incredibly versatile ingredient consisting of a yolk and white (albumen), used for binding, thickening, emulsifying, leavening, adding richness, flavor, and color.

# Flavor and Aroma
* **Flavor Profile:**
    * **White (Albumen):** Largely flavorless when raw, very mild when cooked. Can have slightly sulfureous notes.
    * **Yolk:** Rich, fatty, creamy, savory (umami). Flavor varies slightly depending on the hen's diet.
* **Aroma Profile:**
    * **Raw:** Mild, slightly sulfureous.
    * **Cooked:** Ranges from subtle (poached) to savory and rich (fried), often developing sulfureous notes (especially when overcooked hard-boiled). Browning adds nutty aromas.
* **Key Compounds:**
    * **Yolk:** Lipids (fats), Cholesterol, **Lecithin** (an emulsifier), Carotenoids (color), Proteins, Glutamates (umami).
    * **White:** Primarily **Proteins** (e.g., ovalbumin), Water, Sulfur-containing amino acids (contribute to aroma when cooked).

# Culinary Function
Eggs perform numerous functions, often simultaneously:
* **Binding:** Proteins coagulate when heated, holding ingredients together (e.g., meatballs, meatloaf, cakes).
* **Thickening:** Proteins coagulate to thicken liquids (e.g., custards like crème anglaise, crème brûlée, pastry cream, quiches).
* **Emulsifying:** Lecithin in the yolk allows fat and water-based liquids to mix smoothly without separating (e.g., mayonnaise, hollandaise sauce, Caesar dressing).
* **Leavening:** Whisked egg whites (or whole eggs) trap air bubbles, which expand upon heating, providing lift and volume (e.g., meringues, soufflés, sponge cakes, choux pastry).
* **Adding Richness & Flavor:** Yolks contribute fat, creating a richer mouthfeel and savory flavor (e.g., custards, enriched breads, pasta dough).
* **Adding Color:** Carotenoids in the yolk provide yellow/golden color (e.g., custards, cakes, pasta). Egg wash creates a golden-brown, shiny crust on baked goods.
* **Coating/Glazing:** Used as a wash before baking for color and shine, or as a binder for breadcrumbs (e.g., schnitzel, fried chicken).
* **Clarifying:** Egg whites can trap impurities in liquids like stocks or consommés, which are then strained out.

# Common Forms
* **Whole (in Shell):** The most common form, sold by size (e.g., Medium, Large, Extra Large). Shell color (white/brown) does not affect nutrition or flavor.
* **Liquid Eggs:** Pasteurized whole eggs, whites, or yolks sold in cartons for convenience.
* **Powdered Eggs:** Dehydrated eggs (whole, white, or yolk) used in processed foods or for long-term storage/convenience. Requires rehydration.

# Preparation
* **Cracking:** Tapping firmly on a flat surface is preferred over an edge to reduce shell fragments.
* **Separating:** Carefully cracking and passing the yolk between shell halves, using fingers, or using a dedicated separator to isolate the yolk from the white. Essential for meringues or recipes needing only one component.
* **Whisking/Beating:** Incorporates air (for leavening) or combines yolk and white. Whites reach different stages (soft peak, stiff peak) critical for specific recipes.
* **Common Cooking Methods:** Boiling (soft/hard), Frying (sunny-side up, over easy/medium/hard, basted), Scrambling, Poaching, Baking (shirred eggs, in muffins, cakes, quiches, etc.), Omelettes/Frittatas.

# Pairings and Cuisine
* **Classic Pairings:** **Bacon**, **sausage**, **ham**, **cheese**, **toast/bread**, **potatoes**, **spinach**, **mushrooms**, hollandaise sauce, mayonnaise.
* **Protein Pairings:** Integral part of dishes with bacon, sausage, smoked salmon. Binds ground meats.
* **Spice Pairings:** Salt, **black pepper**, paprika, chili flake, chives, parsley, dill, tarragon.
* **Common Cuisines:** Universal. Found in breakfast dishes, baking, sauces, custards, and main courses across the globe.
    * *Examples:* French Omelette, Quiche Lorraine (French), Eggs Benedict (American), Shakshuka (Middle Eastern/North African), Scotch Egg (British), Tamagoyaki (Japanese), Egg Drop Soup (Chinese), Huevos Rancheros (Mexican), Flan (Global).

# Substitutions
Depends heavily on the egg's function in the recipe:
* **Binding:**
    * **Flax Egg/Chia Egg:** 1 tbsp ground flax/chia + 3 tbsp water, let sit 5-10 min (good for baking, meatballs).
    * Mashed banana, applesauce, silken tofu, starches (potato/corn).
* **Leavening:**
    * **Aquafaba** (liquid from canned chickpeas): Whip like egg whites for meringues/mousses. 3 tbsp ≈ 1 egg white.
    * Chemical leaveners (baking soda + acid like vinegar/lemon juice).
* **Emulsifying:** Silken tofu, soy lecithin, commercial vegan mayonnaise bases.
* **Richness/Moisture:** Mashed banana, applesauce, yogurt, silken tofu, vegetable oil.
* **Commercial Egg Replacers:** Powdered products designed to mimic eggs in baking, follow package instructions.
