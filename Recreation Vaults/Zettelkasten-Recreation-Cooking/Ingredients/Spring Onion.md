**Ingredient:** Spring Onion (Scallion / Green Onion)
**(Optional) Scientific Name:** *Allium fistulosum*

# Summary
An allium with a mild, fresh onion flavor, prized for its dual texture, with a pungent white base and a crisp, herbaceous green top.

# Flavor and Aroma
* **Flavor Profile:** A distinct but mild onion "bite" (less sharp than a bulb onion) and a fresh, "green" herbaceous taste.
    * **Whites/Light Greens:** More pungent, sharper, onion-forward flavor.
    * **Dark Green Tops:** Milder, grassy, and chive-like.
* **Aroma Profile:** Fresh, sharp, and sulfureous when raw.
* **Key Compounds:** **Sulfur-containing compounds** (like those in alliums) provide the characteristic pungency. Cooking methods alter these compounds significantly.

# Culinary Function
* **Primary Role:** A versatile aromatic and garnish. It can provide a sharp, fresh bite when raw or a sweet, mellow onion flavor when cooked.
* **Effect of Heat:**
    * **Raw:** Sharp, pungent, and crisp. Used as a finishing garnish to add "zing" and texture.
    * **Cooked:** Mellows significantly, losing its sharp bite and becoming sweeter and more savory. The whites are often added with garlic/ginger as an aromatic base, while the greens are added at the end of cooking to maintain color and fresh flavor.
* **Interactions:** Cuts through richness and fat. Brightens savory dishes (soups, eggs, starches). Adds a fresh counterpoint to cooked flavors.

# Common Forms
* **Fresh:** Sold in bunches, sometimes with small, undeveloped bulbs at the base. This is the only common form.

# Preparation
* **Wash:** Rinse thoroughly, especially between the green layers where grit can hide.
* **Trim:** Slice off the root hairs at the very bottom and the wilted, dry tips of the green tops.
* **Slicing (Bias):** Slicing on a diagonal (bias) creates more surface area and is a common presentation in Asian cuisine.
* **Slicing (Rounds):** Thinly slicing crosswise ("coins") is common for garnish.
* **Shredding (Curls):** Thinly slicing green tops lengthwise into fine strips and placing them in ice water causes them to curl, creating a decorative garnish.
* **Whole/Halved:** Used for grilling or roasting, which caramelizes the vegetable and makes it sweet.
* **Separation:** Often the white/light green parts are separated from the dark green tops. The whites are cooked with other aromatics (like garlic/ginger) at the beginning of a recipe, while the greens are reserved for the end or used as a raw garnish.

# Pairings and Cuisine
* **Classic Pairings:** **Ginger**, **garlic**, **soy sauce**, sesame oil, chili, tofu, eggs, potatoes.
* **Protein Pairings:** Chicken, pork, beef, fish, tofu, eggs.
* **Spice Pairings:** Cilantro, parsley, white pepper, Szechuan peppercorn.
* **Common Cuisines:**
    * **East Asian (Chinese, Japanese, Korean):** Essential aromatic base (with ginger/garlic), garnish for soups (ramen, pho), stir-fries, dipping sauces, and pancakes (pajeon, cong you bing).
    * **Mexican:** Garnish for tacos, tostadas, and soups.
    * **American:** Garnish for baked potatoes, chili, and dips.

# Substitutions
* **Best Match (for Greens/Garnish):** **Chives** (milder, more delicate, garlicky-onion flavor).
* **Best Match (for Whites/Cooking):** **Shallots** (minced) or the white/light green parts of a **Leek** (milder, sweeter).
* **In a Pinch (Different Profile):**
    * **Red Onion (minced):** Provides a sharp, raw bite (good for garnish in Mexican food).
    * **Yellow/White Onion (minced):** Can substitute the white part in cooked applications, but lacks the green, fresh element.
