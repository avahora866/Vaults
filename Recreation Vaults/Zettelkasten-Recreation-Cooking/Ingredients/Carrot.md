**Ingredient:** Carrot
**(Optional) Scientific Name:** *Daucus carota subsp. sativus*

# Summary
A firm, sweet, and earthy root vegetable prized for its bright color, crisp texture when raw, and ability to add a sweet, aromatic base to cooked dishes.

# Flavor and Aroma
* **Flavor Profile:** Sweet, earthy, woody, and slightly vegetal. Bitterness can be present.
* **Aroma Profile:** Grassy, fresh, and earthy.
* **Key Compounds:**
    * **Terpenoids:** Responsible for the characteristic "carrot" aroma (e.g., α-pinene, caryophyllene).
    * **Sugars (Sucrose):** Provide the primary sweetness.
    * **Falcarindiol:** A polyacetylene that is the main compound responsible for bitterness.

# Culinary Function
* **Primary Role:** Serves as a fundamental aromatic base, a source of sweetness, or a textural component.
    * **Aromatic Base:** Forms the "holy trinity" of **mirepoix** (along with onion and celery) or **soffritto**, providing a deep, sweet, savory foundation for stocks, soups, stews, and braises.
* **Effect of Heat:**
    * **Raw:** Crisp, crunchy, and woody.
    * **Cooked:** Becomes very tender and soft. The flavors concentrate, and the sweetness intensifies as the cell walls break down. Roasting leads to caramelization, enhancing the sweetness further.
* **Interactions:** Balances acidic ingredients (like tomatoes). Adds moisture and sweetness to baked goods (e.g., carrot cake).

# Common Forms
* **Whole:** Sold in bunches (often with tops) or in bags (topped and tailed).
* **"Baby" Carrots:** Not actually young carrots. They are mature carrots that have been cut and lathed into small, rounded pieces. Valued for convenience.
* **Shredded/Grated:** Used raw in salads (e.g., carrot salad, coleslaw) or in baking.
* **Juiced:** Used as a beverage or as a liquid base for soups.
* **Dehydrated:** Used in dried soup mixes.

# Preparation
* **Scrubbing/Peeling:** Peeling is optional for young, thin-skinned carrots but recommended for older, tougher ones, as the peel can be bitter and fibrous. Scrubbing is always necessary.
* **Dicing (Mirepoix):** Finely diced for use in aromatic bases where it will be cooked down.
* **Slicing (Coins/Rounds):** A common cut for glazing, steaming, or boiling as a side dish.
* **Batonnet/Julienne (Sticks):** Cut into sticks for stir-frying, roasting (fries), or raw snacking (crudités).
* **Grating:** Used for salads and baking. Releases the most moisture.

# Pairings and Cuisine
* **Classic Pairings:** **Onion**, **celery**, **butter**, **honey**, **maple syrup**, **ginger**, orange, dill, thyme.
* **Protein Pairings:** Beef (e.g., pot roast, stews), chicken (e.g., stock, soup), lamb.
* **Spice Pairings:** **Cumin**, **coriander**, thyme, parsley, bay leaf, cinnamon, nutmeg (especially in baking).
* **Common Cuisines:**
    * **French:** Essential to mirepoix for stocks, braises, and soups.
    * **Indian:** Used in curries, side dishes (sabzi), and desserts (gajar ka halwa).
    * **North African:** Common in tagines, often paired with cumin and citrus.
    * **American:** A staple in pot roasts, chicken noodle soup, and carrot cake.

# Substitutions
* **Best Match (for Mirepoix/Base):** **Parsnip** (sweeter, nuttier) or **Sweet Potato** (starchier).
* **Best Match (for Roasting):** **Parsnip**, **Sweet Potato**, **Butternut Squash**.
* **For Raw Crunch (Different Profile):** **Jicama**, **Daikon Radish** (more peppery), **Bell Peppers** (less dense).
* **In Baking:** **Zucchini** (adds moisture, less flavor) or **Sweet Potato**.
