**Ingredient:** Cornflour (UK) / Cornstarch (US)
**(Optional) Scientific Name:** Starch from *Zea mays*

# Summary
A fine white powder derived from the endosperm of corn kernels, primarily used as a versatile thickening agent in liquids.

# Flavor and Aroma
* **Flavor Profile:** Neutral and virtually tasteless when properly cooked. Can taste starchy or chalky if undercooked.
* **Aroma Profile:** Neutral.
* **Key Compounds:** **Starch**, composed mainly of **amylose** and **amylopectin**.

# Culinary Function
* **Primary Role:** **Thickening agent** for sauces, gravies, custards, soups, and pie fillings. Also used as an **anti-caking agent** (e.g., in icing sugar) and to create a **crisp coating** for fried foods.
* **Effect of Heat:** Requires mixing with **cold liquid** first (slurry) to prevent lumps, then heating in the main liquid to activate. Gelatinization (thickening) begins around 62°C (144°F) and requires reaching near-boiling temperatures (around 95°C / 203°F) for maximum thickening power. Over-boiling or prolonged heating can break down the starch and cause thinning. Acidic ingredients can also weaken its thickening ability.
* **Interactions:** Creates a translucent, glossy finish when thickening liquids. Provides a light, crispy crust when used as a coating for frying (inhibits gluten development compared to flour). Prevents ice crystals in sorbets and helps stabilize emulsions.

# Common Forms
* **Powder:** Fine, white, silky powder. This is the standard form.

# Preparation
* **Slurry:** **Essential Step:** Always mix cornflour with an equal amount of **cold** water, stock, or other liquid until completely smooth *before* adding it to a hot liquid. Stir the slurry constantly while slowly pouring it into the simmering liquid you wish to thicken. Bring back to a simmer and cook for 1-2 minutes, stirring, until thickened and the raw starch taste is gone.
* **Coating:** Used dry, often mixed with flour and seasonings, to coat ingredients (e.g., chicken, tofu, vegetables) before frying for a crispy texture.
* **Dusting:** Lightly dusted on surfaces to prevent sticking, though less common than flour for this.

# Pairings and Cuisine
* **Classic Pairings:** Works with nearly any liquid needing thickening: stock/broth (gravies, soups), fruit juice (pie fillings, glazes), dairy (custards, puddings), sugar syrups (confectionery).
* **Protein Pairings:** Used as a coating for chicken, pork, tofu, fish, and seafood, particularly in stir-frying techniques (velveting).
* **Spice Pairings:** N/A (neutral flavor carrier).
* **Common Cuisines:** Global.
    * **East Asian (especially Chinese):** Essential for thickening stir-fry sauces, velveting meats, and making crispy coatings.
    * **Western:** Used in pastry cream (crème pâtissière), fruit pie fillings, puddings, gravies, and sauces.

# Substitutions
* **Note:** Different thickeners have varying properties (clarity, thickening temperature, stability).
* **Best Match (similar properties):** **Arrowroot** (yields a clearer, glossier sauce, thickens at lower temp, less stable to reheating/prolonged heat), **Potato Starch** (very clear, thickens at lower temp, can be slightly gluey if overused), **Tapioca Starch** (glossy, thickens quickly, can be stringy). Use slightly less or similar amounts.
* **In a Pinch (different properties):** **All-Purpose Flour** (makes sauces opaque and less glossy, requires longer cooking to remove raw taste, has less thickening power per tablespoon, contains gluten). Use roughly twice the amount of flour for the same thickening power as cornflour.
* **Ratio:** 1 tablespoon cornflour ≈ 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour.
