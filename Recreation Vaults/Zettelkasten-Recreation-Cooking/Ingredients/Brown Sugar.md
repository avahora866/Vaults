**Ingredient:** Brown Sugar
**(Optional) Scientific Name:** *Sucrose* (with molasses)

# Summary
A sweetener made from sucrose and molasses, providing a moist texture and a deep, caramel-like flavor that white sugar lacks.

# Flavor and Aroma
* **Flavor Profile:** Rich, deep, complex sweetness. Notes of caramel, toffee, and molasses. Dark brown sugar is more robust and molasses-forward than light brown sugar.
* **Aroma Profile:** Sweet, treacly, toffee-like.
* **Key Compounds:** The flavor comes from the molasses content. This includes compounds from the **Maillard reaction** and caramelization that occurred during sugarcane processing (e.t., **maltol**, **isomaltol**, and various pyrones), which provide roasted, caramel, and toasty notes.

# Culinary Function
* **Primary Role:** Sweetener, flavor-enhancer, and humectant (adds moisture).
* **Effect of Heat:** Melts and caramelizes readily, contributing to a deep brown color in baked goods, sauces, and glazes.
* **Interactions:**
    * **Baking:** The molasses content is acidic, which reacts with alkaline leaveners like baking soda (*sodium bicarbonate*) to produce carbon dioxide gas, aiding in leavening.
    * **Moisture:** It is hygroscopic (attracts water), which makes baked goods like cookies softer, moister, and chewier than those made with white sugar alone.
    * **Flavor:** Adds a complex, roasted sweetness to BBQ sauces, glazes for meat (like ham), and baked beans.

# Common Forms
* **Light Brown Sugar:** The most common form. Contains ~3.5% molasses. Used when a subtle caramel note is desired (e.g., most chocolate chip cookies, butterscotch).
* **Dark Brown Sugar:** Contains ~6.5% molasses. Used when a stronger, more robust molasses flavor is needed (e.g., gingerbread, baked beans, glazes).
* **Muscovado (Light and Dark):** An unrefined or partially refined cane sugar where the molasses is not added back, but rather retained. It is stickier, coarser, and has a much stronger, more complex molasses flavor.
* **Demerara/Turbinado:** Coarse, "raw" sugar crystals with a light molasses coating. Less moist than standard brown sugar.

# Preparation
* **Measuring:** Due to its moisture and air pockets, brown sugar must be **"packed"** into a measuring cup unless a recipe specifies otherwise (e.g., by weight). This means pressing the sugar firmly into the cup to remove air and ensure a consistent volume.
* **Softening:** If hardened, it can be softened by placing a piece of bread, a damp paper towel, or a terra cotta brown sugar saver in an airtight container with it.

# Pairings and Cuisine
* **Classic Pairings:** **Cinnamon**, **pecans**, **walnuts**, **apples**, **pumpkin**, **oats**, **butter**, **bacon**, **mustard**.
* **Protein Pairings:** Pork (ham, bacon, pulled pork), salmon (glazes), chicken (glazes).
* **Spice Pairings:** Cinnamon, nutmeg, cloves, allspice, ginger.
* **Common Cuisines:**
    * **American:** Chocolate chip cookies, BBQ sauce, baked beans, pecan pie, oatmeal.
    * **Global:** Used in countless glazes, marinades, and desserts.

# Substitutions
* **DIY Light Brown Sugar:** Mix 1 cup (200g) granulated white sugar with 1 tablespoon (15ml) molasses.
* **DIY Dark Brown Sugar:** Mix 1 cup (200g) granulated white sugar with 2 tablespoons (30ml) molasses.
* **In a Pinch:** Granulated white sugar (will change texture; baked goods will be crisper and flatter), maple syrup (will add liquid), or coconut sugar. Muscovado sugar can be substituted 1:1, but provides a stronger flavor.
