**Ingredient:** Cucumber
**(Optional) Scientific Name:** *Cucumis sativus*

# Summary
A crisp, high-water-content fruit (used as a vegetable) that adds a cooling, refreshing crunch and mild flavor to dishes.

# Flavor and Aroma
* **Flavor Profile:** Mildly sweet, "green," vegetal, aqueous, refreshing. The skin and ends can be bitter.
* **Aroma Profile:** Fresh, clean, grassy, melon-like.
* **Key Compounds:**
    * **(E,Z)-nona-2,6-dienal** & **(E)-non-2-enal:** Aldehydes responsible for the characteristic "green" cucumber aroma.
    * **Cucurbitacins:** A class of compounds that cause bitterness, concentrated at the stem end and just under the skin. Modern varieties are bred for low bitterness.

# Culinary Function
* **Primary Role:** Adds crisp texture, freshness, and coolness. Almost exclusively used raw.
* **Effect of Heat:** Loses all crispness rapidly. Becomes soft, translucent, and watery. Flavor becomes bland. Heat is generally avoided, though quick stir-frying is used in some Asian dishes.
* **Interactions:** Provides a cooling, hydrating contrast to spicy, hot, or rich foods (e.g., curry, chili). Dilutes strong flavors.

# Common Forms
* **English (Hothouse):** Long, narrow, dark green, thin-skinned, and nearly seedless. Often sold wrapped in plastic as the skin is delicate and unwaxed.
* **Slicing (American):** Shorter, thicker, with a tough, waxy dark green skin and a more prominent, watery seed core.
* **Kirby (Pickling):** Short, firm, bumpy, and crisp. The standard variety used for making pickles (gherkins).
* **Persian:** Small, thin-skinned, crisp, and very mild, with minimal seeds.

# Preparation
* **Peeling:** Optional for thin-skinned types (English, Persian). Often required for Slicing cucumbers to remove the bitter, waxy skin.
* **Seeding:** Slice lengthwise and use a small spoon to scrape out the watery seed core. This is critical for preventing salads or sauces (like tzatziki) from becoming soggy.
* **Salting (Degorging):** Tossing sliced or diced cucumber with salt (maceration) draws out excess water via osmosis. Let sit for 20-30 minutes, then rinse and pat dry. This concentrates flavor and improves texture.
* **Grated:** Used for sauces like tzatziki or raita. Must be squeezed dry after grating.
* **Sliced/Diced/Julienned:** Used for salads, salsas, and sandwiches.

# Pairings and Cuisine
* **Classic Pairings:** **Yogurt**, **dill**, **mint**, lemon, garlic, red onion, gin, rice vinegar.
* **Protein Pairings:** Salmon, white fish, chicken, tofu.
* **Spice Pairings:** Black pepper, chili flake, mustard seed (in pickles), sesame seeds, Aleppo pepper.
* **Common Cuisines:** Greek (tzatziki, Greek salad), Indian (raita), British (sandwiches, Pimm's cup), Japanese (sunomono, pickled), Middle Eastern (shirazi salad).

# Substitutions
* **Best Match (Raw):** **Raw Zucchini** (especially summer squash, very similar mild flavor and texture).
* **For Crunch (Different Profile):** **Celery** (more fibrous, stronger flavor), **Jicama** (sweeter), **Green Bell Pepper** (stronger flavor).
* **For Coolness/Mildness:** Unripe honeydew or cantaloupe melon.
