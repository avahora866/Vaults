**Ingredient:** Mushroom
**(Optional) Scientific Name:** Kingdom Fungi (specifically macroscopic fruiting bodies of various species, e.g., *Agaricus bisporus* for common button/cremini/portobello)

# Summary
A versatile fungus prized for its savory umami flavor, unique earthy notes, and often meaty texture, used in a wide array of culinary applications.

# Flavor and Aroma
* **Flavor Profile:** Earthy, savory (umami), brothy, sometimes nutty, woody, or peppery depending on the variety. Can range from delicate (button) to intensely rich (shiitake, porcini).
* **Aroma Profile:** Earthy, loamy, forest floor, damp soil. Cooked mushrooms develop deeper roasted or savory aromas.
* **Key Compounds:**
    * **Glutamates:** A major source of natural **umami** taste.
    * **Guanosine Monophosphate (GMP):** Another powerful umami compound, works synergistically with glutamates. Found especially high in dried shiitake.
    * **1-octen-3-ol:** A key volatile alcohol responsible for the characteristic "mushroomy" aroma.
    * **Sulfur Compounds:** Contribute to the savory depth.

# Culinary Function
* **Primary Role:** Adds umami depth, provides a 'meaty' texture (especially larger varieties like Portobello), absorbs flavors well, and adds moisture (they release water when cooked).
* **Effect of Heat:**
    * **Raw:** Mild flavor, often crisp or slightly spongy texture (e.g., button mushrooms in salads).
    * **Cooked:** Intensifies the earthy and umami flavors. Texture becomes tender, soft, and slightly chewy. Sautéing or roasting drives off water, concentrating flavor and promoting browning (Maillard reaction), adding nutty/roasted notes. Boiling can make them watery and dilute flavor.
* **Interactions:** Act like sponges, readily absorbing fats (oil, butter) and liquids (stock, wine, sauces). Pairs exceptionally well with savory ingredients, herbs, and dairy.

# Common Forms
* **Fresh:** Sold whole or sliced. Various types available:
    * **Button:** Young *Agaricus bisporus*. Mildest flavor.
    * **Cremini (Baby Bella):** Slightly older *Agaricus bisporus*. Browner, firmer, more flavor than button.
    * **Portobello:** Fully mature *Agaricus bisporus*. Large cap, intensely savory, meaty texture.
    * **Shiitake:** Savory, smoky, umami-rich. Firm texture. Stems are usually tough and removed (used for stock).
    * **Oyster:** Delicate, slightly sweet, velvety texture. Grow in clusters.
    * **Enoki:** Long, thin stems with tiny caps. Mild, slightly crisp. Often used in soups and hot pots.
    * **Chanterelle:** Fruity (apricot-like) aroma, peppery taste. Golden color.
    * **Morel:** Honeycombed cap. Deeply earthy, nutty, and highly prized. Must be cooked.
* **Dried:** Flavor is highly concentrated and intensified, especially umami. Need rehydrating before use; the soaking liquid is very flavorful and often used in stocks or sauces (e.g., dried shiitake, porcini).
* **Canned/Jarred:** Cooked and preserved in brine. Often have a softer texture and less intense flavor than fresh or dried.
* **Powdered:** Dried mushrooms ground into powder. Used as an umami seasoning or flavor base.

# Preparation
* **Cleaning:** **Avoid soaking**. Mushrooms absorb water. Brush off dirt with a soft brush or paper towel. If necessary, quickly rinse and pat thoroughly dry. Trim off the dry stem ends.
* **Slicing/Dicing:** Cut as needed for the recipe. Uniform sizes ensure even cooking.
* **Sautéing:** Cook in hot fat (butter/oil) without overcrowding the pan (allows moisture to evaporate and promotes browning rather than steaming).
* **Roasting:** Toss with oil and seasoning, roast at high temperature until browned and tender. Concentrates flavor.
* **Grilling:** Larger mushrooms (like Portobello) are excellent grilled, often marinated first.

# Pairings and Cuisine
* **Classic Pairings:** **Garlic**, **butter**, **thyme**, **parsley**, **cream**, **onions/shallots**, **red wine**, **white wine**, eggs, bacon, cheese (Parmesan, Gruyère), beef.
* **Protein Pairings:** Beef, chicken, eggs, bacon, tofu, lentils.
* **Spice Pairings:** Thyme, rosemary, sage, black pepper, paprika, nutmeg.
* **Common Cuisines:** Found globally.
    * **French:** Duxelles, sauces, stews (Boeuf Bourguignon), omelettes.
    * **Italian:** Pasta sauces, risotto, pizza toppings.
    * **Chinese/Japanese:** Stir-fries, soups, stocks (especially shiitake).
    * **Eastern European:** Soups (borscht), sauces, fillings for pierogi.

# Substitutions
* **For Umami:** Dried mushrooms/powder, tomato paste, soy sauce, nutritional yeast, Parmesan cheese.
* **For Meaty Texture:** **Eggplant (Aubergine)**, firm **Tofu**, **Tempeh**, **Lentils**, **Beans**, **Seitan**.
* **Variety Substitution:** Cremini can substitute button (more flavor), Portobello can substitute Cremini (meatiest). Shiitake have a distinct flavor not easily replaced. Mild Oyster mushrooms can replace Button/Cremini in many dishes.
