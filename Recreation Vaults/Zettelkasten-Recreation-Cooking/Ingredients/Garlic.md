**Ingredient:** Garlic
**(Optional) Scientific Name:** *Allium sativum*

# Summary
A pungent bulb from the allium family used as a fundamental aromatic base to add a sharp, sulfureous bite when raw or a deep, sweet, and nutty flavor when cooked.

# Flavor and Aroma
* **Flavor Profile:**
    * **Raw:** Pungent, sharp, sulfureous, and spicy.
    * **Cooked:** Mellows significantly, becoming nutty, savory (umami), and sweet (especially when roasted).
* **Aroma Profile:** Sharp, sulfureous, and penetrating.
* **Key Compounds:**
    * **Alliin:** An odorless compound present in intact cloves.
    * **Alliinase:** An enzyme stored separately from alliin in the clove.
    * **Allicin:** The potent, pungent compound responsible for the characteristic "garlic" aroma and heat. It is **not** present in whole garlic. It is produced *only* when the clove's cells are damaged (crushed, minced, sliced), allowing alliinase to react with alliin. Allicin is unstable and breaks down into other sulfur compounds.

# Culinary Function
* **Primary Role:** A core aromatic, providing a savory foundation for countless dishes. Its flavor is highly dependent on preparation.
* **Effect of Preparation (Cell Damage):** The more cell damage, the more allicin is created, and the sharper the flavor.
    * **Whole Clove:** Very mild, sweet, and nutty when cooked (e.g., roasted).
    * **Sliced:** Mild, balanced garlic flavor.
    * **Crushed (Smashed):** Robust, strong flavor.
    * **Minced/Grated/Paste:** Most intense, pungent, and "spicy" flavor, as cell damage is maximized.
* **Effect of Heat:** Heat destroys allicin and tames the pungency.
    * **Raw:** Sharp and aggressive (e.g., pesto, aioli, dressings).
    * **Lightly Cooked (Sautéed):** Becomes aromatic and nutty. It burns very quickly, turning acrid and bitter.
    * **Slowly Cooked (Roasted/Confit):** Becomes soft, creamy, and deeply sweet as the sugars caramelize.

# Common Forms
* **Fresh:** Sold as a whole bulb or as individual cloves.
* **Garlic Powder:** Dehydrated, ground garlic. Has a different, toastier, and less sharp flavor. Disperses easily in liquids and dry rubs.
* **Granulated Garlic:** A coarser grind than garlic powder, often used in rubs.
* **Jarred Minced:** Minced garlic preserved in water or oil. Less pungent than fresh and can have a tangy or "off" preservative flavor.
* **Black Garlic:** Whole bulbs aged under controlled humidity and heat, causing the cloves to turn black, soft, and jelly-like. The flavor is sweet, balsamic, and savory, with no raw garlic pungency.
* **Garlic Scapes:** The green, flowering stalks from hardneck garlic. Crisp and milder, with a fresh garlic/spring onion flavor.

# Preparation
* **Peeling:** Smash a clove with the flat side of a chef's knife to easily break and remove the papery skin.
* **Slicing:** Sliced thinly for infusing oil or for visible pieces in stir-fries.
* **Crushing/Smashing:** Done to release flavor for a soup, stock, or pan sauce; the clove is often removed later.
* **Mincing:** Finely chopping, creating a strong flavor that dissolves into the dish. Adding salt while mincing helps create a paste.
* **Grating:** Using a microplane creates a fine, wet paste that is extremely pungent, ideal for raw applications like dressings.
* **Roasting:** Whole heads (tops sliced off, drizzled with oil, wrapped in foil) are roasted until soft.

# Pairings and Cuisine
* **Classic Pairings:** **Olive oil**, **butter**, **onion**, **ginger**, **tomato**, **lemon**, **parsley**, **rosemary**, **thyme**.
* **Protein Pairings:** Virtually all; **chicken**, **beef**, **lamb**, **shrimp**, **tofu**, **beans**.
* **Spice Pairings:** Black pepper, chili flake, cumin, coriander.
* **Common Cuisines:** Global staple.
    * **Italian:** Essential base (soffritto, aglio e olio, pesto).
    * **French:** Essential base (mirepoix, persillade).
    * **Chinese:** Essential base (often with ginger and spring onion).
    * **Indian:** Essential base for curries and masalas.
    * **Mexican:** Essential base for salsas and adobos.
    * **Middle Eastern:** Used in dips, marinades, and sauces (e.g., toum).

# Substitutions
* **Best Match (for Cooked Aromatic):** **Shallots** (milder, sweeter onion-garlic flavor).
* **For Raw Pungency:** **Spring Onion (whites)** or **Garlic Scapes** (when in season).
* **Dry:** 1 fresh clove ≈ 1/4 teaspoon **garlic powder**.
* **In a Pinch (Different Profile):** **Asafoetida (Hing)**. A pinch, bloomed in hot oil, provides a sulfureous, allium-like background note.
