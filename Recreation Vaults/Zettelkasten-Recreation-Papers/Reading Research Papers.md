# The Three-Pass Approach

This method helps you efficiently extract information based on your goals.

**First Pass: The Overview (5-10 minutes)**
The goal is to get a high-level understanding of the paper.

1.  Read the **Title**, **Abstract**, and **Introduction**.
2.  Read the section and sub-section **headings**.
3.  Examine any **figures**, **tables**, and **graphs**. Read their captions.
4.  Read the **Conclusion**.

After this pass, you should be able to answer:
* What problem does this paper try to solve?
* What is the main contribution or result?
* Is this paper relevant to my goals?

**Second Pass: The Content (Approx. 1 hour)**
The goal is to grasp the paper's content, but not its fine details.

1.  Read the paper from start to finish.
2.  Focus on understanding the **methodology**, **arguments**, and **evidence**.
3.  Make notes as you read. Write down questions.
4.  *Do not* get stuck on complex math, proofs, or unfamiliar terms. Mark them and move on. Try to understand them from context.

After this pass, you should be able to summarize the paper's main points and supporting evidence to someone else.

**Third Pass: The Details (Several hours)**
The goal is to understand the paper completely. This pass is necessary if you need to critique, build upon, or implement the work.

1.  Re-read the paper, focusing on the parts you marked in Pass 2.
2.  Work through the mathematics and proofs step-by-step.
3.  Challenge the authors' assumptions and methodology.
4.  Cross-reference the citations. Read important cited papers to understand the background context.

# Handling Confusing Terminology

This is often the biggest barrier.

* **Start with Review Papers:** Before reading novel research, find "review" articles or "surveys" on the topic. These papers synthesize the field, define key terms, and provide context. This is highly effective for new domains like quantum computing or specific finance models.
* **Build a Glossary:** Keep a running document for each subject (e.g., Quantum Algorithms, ML Models). Write down new terms, their symbols (common in math/physics), and their definitions.
* **Focus on Context:** In your second pass, do not stop to look up every word. You can often infer meaning from the surrounding text. Only look up terms that completely block your understanding of a key section.
* **Read Foundational Papers:** In any field, 5-10 foundational papers introduce the core ideas and terminology. Identify and read these first.

# Applying to Your Fields

* **Math, Physics, and Quantum:** These papers are definition- and proof-heavy. The terminology is precise and sequential. You must understand Definition A before you can understand Theorem B. The third pass is essential.
* **Machine Learning:** These papers often introduce a new architecture or algorithm. Focus on the methodology (Pass 2) and the implementation details (Pass 3).
* **Finance:** Quantitative finance papers resemble physics or math papers. Theoretical economics papers are more argumentative. The same reading strategy applies.

Reading papers becomes faster with practice. The first few in a new field are the most difficult.
