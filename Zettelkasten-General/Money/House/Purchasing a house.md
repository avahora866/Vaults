#  Budgeting & Financing
- Deposit: Typically, you need at least 5-10% of the property price for a deposit. A larger deposit (e.g., 20-25%) can unlock better mortgage rates.
	- For reference if u want a 250k house that js 50,000 and 15% is 37,500.
- [[Mortgage]] Options:
    - Fixed-rate mortgages (stable monthly payments)
    - Variable-rate mortgages (fluctuate with interest rates)
    - Help to Buy & Shared Ownership schemes may be available.
- Affordability Check: Lenders assess income, expenses, and credit score before approving a mortgage.

#  Government Schemes for First-Time Buyers
- Lifetime ISA (LISA): The government adds a 25% bonus to savings (up to £1,000 per year).
- First Homes Scheme: Discounts of 30-50% on new-build homes for eligible buyers.
- Stamp Duty Relief: First-time buyers pay no stamp duty on properties up to £425,000.

#  Understanding Freehold vs. Leasehold
- Freehold: You own both the house and the land it sits on.
- Leasehold: You own the house but not the land, meaning you pay ground rent and service charges (common for flats but can apply to houses).

#  Legal & Fees
- Conveyancing Fees: A solicitor or conveyancer handles legal paperwork, costing £800-£2,000.
- Stamp Duty: If your house costs over £425,000, you’ll pay 5% on the portion between £425,001 - £625,000.
- Surveys & Valuations: These cost £250-£1,500, depending on the survey type.
- Other Costs: Mortgage arrangement fees, home insurance, and moving costs.

#  Property Surveys & Renovation Considerations
- Surveys:
    - Basic Valuation – Required by lenders but doesn’t check for structural issues.
    - Homebuyer’s Report – Assesses the condition of the property.
    - Full Structural Survey – Recommended for older or renovated homes.
- Renovation Costs: Check for damp, structural issues, outdated wiring, and insulation needs. Consider the cost of extensions or modernizing outdated rooms (kitchen, bathrooms).

#  Key Considerations Before Buying
- Location & Amenities: Schools, transport links, and local services affect property value.
- Future Resale Value: Consider long-term appreciation potential.
- Check for Planning Permissions: Ensure there are no restrictions on modifications.
