The concept of a **chain house** (or **property chain**) refers to a series of property transactions that are linked together because each purchase depends on the sale of another property. It typically occurs in residential real estate when multiple buyers and sellers are connected in a sequence, each waiting for their own sale to complete before they can proceed with their purchase.

# How a Property Chain Works

1. **Buyer 1** (First-time buyer or cash buyer) purchases a house from **Seller 1**.
2. **Seller 1** is also buying a home from **Seller 2**, and they need to sell their property first to fund their purchase.
3. **Seller 2** might also be buying another property, continuing the chain.

If any sale in the chain is delayed or falls through (e.g., due to financing issues, failed surveys, or buyer withdrawal), it can disrupt the entire chain, causing delays or cancellations for everyone involved.

# Chain-Free Properties

- **New-build homes** – These are often sold directly by developers, avoiding a chain.
- **Cash buyers** – Buyers who don’t need to sell a property before purchasing.
- **Vacant properties** – Homes that don’t rely on an onward purchase.

# Challenges of a Property Chain

- **Delays & uncertainty** – One weak link can affect multiple transactions.
- **Increased risk of collapse** – If one buyer/seller backs out, others may have to renegotiate or restart their property search.
- **Longer completion times** – More parties mean more legal and financial coordination.
