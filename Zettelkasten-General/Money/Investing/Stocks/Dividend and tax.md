# Dividends and Capital Gains: Separate Taxes

- Dividends represent income you receive as a shareholder when a company distributes profits.
    - Taxed under Income Tax rules.
    - They are not included when calculating capital gains from selling shares.
- Capital Gains Tax (CGT) applies only to the profit made when you sell an asset, such as shares, for more than you paid for it.

---

# How Dividends Are Taxed in the UK (2023/24 Rules):

- Each taxpayer has a Dividend Allowance, which is tax-free.
    - For 2023/24, the allowance is £1,000.
    - Any dividends above this amount are subject to Income Tax.
- Tax rates depend on your Income Tax band:
    - Basic Rate (20%): Dividend tax is 8.75%.
    - Higher Rate (40%): Dividend tax is 33.75%.
    - Additional Rate (45%): Dividend tax is 39.35%.
- Dividends received within a Stocks and Shares ISA are tax-free.
    

---

# Practical Example:

- You hold shares that pay £2,000 in dividends during the year.
    - The first £1,000 is tax-free (dividend allowance).
    - The remaining £1,000 is taxed at your applicable Income Tax rate.
- Separately, you sell some shares for a gain of £5,000.
    - If your total gains exceed the CGT allowance, you'll pay tax on the excess.

# Tax Implications of Reinvesting Dividends

- Dividends are taxed as income at the time they are paid, even if you reinvest them back into the same stock or a different investment.
- Reinvesting dividends does not exempt them from Income Tax or count toward Capital Gains Tax (CGT) allowances.

## Example:

- You receive £1,500 in dividends.
- You reinvest the full amount to buy more shares of the same company.
- Tax liability:
    - £1,000 is covered by the Dividend Allowance (2023/24).
    - The remaining £500 is subject to your dividend tax rate (e.g., 8.75%, 33.75%, or 39.35%).

---

## Reinvested Dividends and Capital Gains Tax (CGT)

- Reinvested dividends increase your cost basis for CGT purposes when you eventually sell the shares.
- The cost basis of the shares will include the amount you paid to purchase them, including reinvested dividends.

# Tax-Sheltered Accounts

To avoid paying tax on dividends or reinvested dividends:
- Use a Stocks and Shares ISA in the UK. Dividends and capital gains within an ISA are completely tax-free
- Use a SIPP (Self-Invested Personal Pension) for long-term, tax-advantaged investing.
