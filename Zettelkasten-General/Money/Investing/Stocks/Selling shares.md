# If I buy 1 share of a stock at 10 then wait a month and buy one more share of the same stock at 20. If I then sell the stock when the price went up by 10. Will the selling be of the first stock gain of 20 or the second stock gain of 10

The answer depends on the accounting method used to determine which stock you are selling. Here are the possibilities:

1. FIFO (First In, First Out)
	- The first stock purchased (at $10) is considered sold first.
	- Selling when the stock price is up by $10 means the selling price is $30.
	- Gain on the first stock: $30 (sale price) - $10 (purchase price) = $20.
2. LIFO (Last In, First Out)
	- The most recent stock purchased (at $20) is considered sold first.
	- Selling when the stock price is up by $10 means the selling price is $30.
	- Gain on the second stock: $30 (sale price) - $20 (purchase price) = $10.
3. Specific Identification
	- If your broker allows it, you can specify which stock (the $10 or $20 one) you are selling.
	- If you choose the first stock: Gain is $20.
	- If you choose the second stock: Gain is $10.
4. Average Cost Basis
	- The cost basis is the average of the two purchase prices:
		($10 + $20) ÷ 2 = $15 per share.
	- Selling at $30 results in a gain of $30 - $15 = $15 per share.
	- Check with your broker or tax advisor to see which method applies to your situation, as it could affect your taxable gains.

## Trading 212

Trading 212 employs the average cost basis method for calculating the cost of shares in your portfolio. This means that when you purchase shares of the same stock at different prices, the platform calculates the average purchase price to determine your position's cost basis. When you sell shares, the gain or loss is calculated based on this average cost, rather than on a First-In-First-Out (FIFO) or Last-In-First-Out (LIFO) basis.

### Example:

First Purchase: 1 share at $10
Second Purchase: 1 share at $20

The average cost per share would be: (£10 + £20) / 2 shares = £15

If you sell 1 share when the stock price is $30, your gain would be:
Selling Price - Average Cost = Gain
£30 - £15 = £15

This approach simplifies the calculation of gains and losses, especially for tax purposes, as it doesn't require tracking the specific order in which shares were purchased and sold.

It's important to note that while Trading 212 uses the average cost method for displaying your portfolio performance, tax authorities in different countries may have specific rules regarding cost basis calculations. For instance, the UK tax system often uses the Section 104 holding method, which is similar to the average cost basis. However, other countries might require FIFO or LIFO methods for tax reporting.

Therefore, it's advisable to consult with a tax professional or refer to your local tax regulations to ensure compliance with the appropriate cost basis reporting method for your jurisdiction.
