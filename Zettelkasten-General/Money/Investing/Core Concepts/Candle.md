A **candle** (or **candlestick**) is a graphical representation of a stock’s price movement over a specific period—commonly used in technical analysis and charting. Each candle shows **four key data points** for that time interval:

# Components of a Candlestick

For any given time period (like 1 minute, 15 minutes, 1 hour, 1 day), a candlestick displays:
* **Open**: The price at the beginning of the period
* **High**: The highest price reached during the period
* **Low**: The lowest price during the period
* **Close**: The price at the end of the period

# The Candlestick Structure
- *Body**: The thick part of the candle, representing the range between open and close.
	- *Green (or white) candle**: Close \> Open (price went up)
	- *Red (or black) candle**: Close \< Open (price went down)
- *Wicks (or shadows)**: The thin lines above and below the body, representing the high and low prices.

# Example: 15-Minute Candle
* The candle starts when a 15-minute interval begins (e.g., 10:00 AM).
* The **open** is the price at 10:00 AM.
* The **high** is the highest price between 10:00–10:15 AM.
* The **low** is the lowest price between 10:00–10:15 AM.
* The **close** is the price at 10:15 AM.

# Why Candles Matter in Your Strategy
Most technical indicators (like RSI, MACD, EMA) are calculated using candle data, not tick-by-tick prices. This makes candles critical for:

* **Detecting trends**
* **Spotting reversal patterns**
* **Timing entries/exits**
