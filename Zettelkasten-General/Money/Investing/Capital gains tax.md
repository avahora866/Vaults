Capital Gains Tax (CGT) is a tax you pay on the profit (gain) you make when you sell or dispose of an asset that has increased in value. You are taxed on the gain, not the total amount you receive.

For example:

- You buy Bitcoin for £5,000.
- You sell it later for £8,000.
- Your gain (profit) is £3,000 (£8,000 - £5,000).

If your total gains exceed the annual CGT allowance, you’ll need to pay CGT on the excess.

# When Do You Pay Capital Gains Tax?

You may need to pay CGT when you:

1. Sell cryptocurrency, stocks, property (excluding your main home), or other assets.
2. Exchange one asset for another (e.g., trading Bitcoin for Ethereum).
3. Gift assets to someone (excluding your spouse/civil partner).
4. Receive compensation (e.g., insurance payouts for a destroyed asset).

Simply holding or transferring assets between your own wallets does not trigger CGT.

# How is CGT Calculated?

1. Calculate Your Gain:
	- Gain = Sale Price - Purchase Price - Allowable Costs
	- Sale Price: The amount you sold the asset for.
	- Purchase Price: What you originally paid for it.
	- Allowable Costs: Fees like transaction fees or improvement costs for property.
2. Apply the CGT Allowance:
	- For the 2023/24 tax year, the allowance is £6,000.
	- Only gains above this amount are taxed.
3. Determine Your CGT Rate:
	- Your income determines the CGT rate:
	- Basic Rate Taxpayers (income up to £50,270): 10% CGT.
	- Higher/Additional Rate Taxpayers (income above £50,270): 20% CGT.

---

# Example Calculation

- You earn £40,000 annually.
- You sell Ethereum, making a gain of £10,000.
- Subtract the CGT allowance: £10,000 - £6,000 = £4,000 taxable gain.

Since your total income + gains (£40,000 + £4,000 = £44,000) is within the basic rate band, you pay 10% CGT:

10% of £4,000 = £400 CGT owed.

---

# When Don’t You Pay CGT?

- Gains within the £6,000 annual allowance
- Gains from selling your main home.
- Gifts to a spouse/civil partner.

# How to Report and Pay CGT?

1. Record all transactions and calculate gains.
2. Report gains through your Self-Assessment Tax Return or via the HMRC Capital Gains Tax online service.
3. Pay the tax by the deadline (31 January after the tax year ends).


# How Losses Offset Gains

1. Claiming the Loss:
	- The loss from selling a stock (or other assets) can be deducted from your capital gains in the same tax year.
	- For example:
		- You make a £10,000 gain from selling Bitcoin.
		- You also sell stocks for a £3,000 loss.
		- Your taxable gain is reduced to £7,000 (£10,000 - £3,000).
2. Carrying Losses Forward:
	- If your total losses exceed your gains in a tax year, the unused losses can be carried forward to future years.
	- You must report these losses to HMRC to carry them forward.

## How to Report Losses

1. Automatic Deduction:
	- HMRC automatically deducts any losses in the current tax year from your gains.
2. Reporting Unused Losses:
	- Even if you have no gains to offset in the current year, you should report losses to HMRC within four years of the end of the tax year.
	- This ensures you can use them in future years when you do have gains.
3. Example of Carrying Forward Losses:
	- In 2023/24, you have:
	- A £4,000 loss from selling stocks.
	- No gains in the same year.
	- You report the £4,000 loss to HMRC.
	- In 2024/25, you make a £10,000 gain from selling crypto.
	- You can deduct the £4,000 loss carried forward, reducing your taxable gain to £6,000.

## Important Notes

Losses can only offset capital gains, not other types of income (like salary).

You can’t claim a loss for assets you give away, except when gifting to a charity.

Losses must be realized (i.e., you must sell the asset). Unrealized losses (holding an asset that has decreased in value) don’t count.
