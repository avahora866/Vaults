In the United Kingdom, the taxation of cryptocurrency depends on the nature of your transactions and the income generated. Here's an overview:

# Capital Gains Tax (CGT):

You may be liable to pay CGT when you "dispose" of your cryptocurrency. Disposal includes:
- Selling cryptoassets for traditional currency.
- Exchanging one cryptoasset for another.
- Using cryptoassets to pay for goods or services.
- Gifting cryptoassets to another person (excluding gifts to a spouse or civil partner).

For the 2024/25 tax year, the Annual Exempt Amount for capital gains is £3,000. This means you can make up to £3,000 in capital gains before CGT applies. If your total gains exceed this allowance, the CGT rates are:

- Basic rate taxpayers: 10% on gains above the allowance.
- Higher and additional rate taxpayers: 20% on gains above the allowance.


# Income Tax:

Certain cryptocurrency activities are subject to Income Tax. These include:

- Mining: The value of mined cryptoassets at the time of receipt is considered taxable income. Subsequent disposal of these assets may also attract CGT.
- Staking and Lending: Earnings from staking or lending cryptoassets can be considered income and taxed accordingly. The specific tax treatment depends on the nature of the activities and should be assessed on a case-by-case basis.
- Airdrops: If you receive cryptoassets through airdrops, their value at the time of receipt may be subject to Income Tax, especially if received in exchange for services or as part of a promotional activity.


# Income Tax rates for the 2024/25 tax year are:

- Personal Allowance: Up to £12,570 – 0% tax.
- Basic Rate: £12,571 to £50,270 – 20% tax.
- Higher Rate: £50,271 to £125,140 – 40% tax.
- Additional Rate: Over £125,140 – 45% tax.

# Record Keeping:
It's essential to maintain detailed records of all cryptocurrency transactions, including dates, amounts, transaction types, and the value in GBP at the time of each transaction. Accurate record-keeping ensures correct tax calculations and compliance with HMRC requirements.

# Reporting and Deadlines:

Taxpayers must report taxable cryptocurrency transactions to HMRC. Deadlines for tax returns are:
- Paper returns: 31 October following the end of the tax year.#
- Online returns: 31 January following the end of the tax year.

For example, for the 2024/25 tax year ending on 5 April 2025, the deadlines are 31 October 2025 (paper) and 31 January 2026 (online).

Note: Tax regulations can change, and individual circumstances vary. It's advisable to consult with a tax professional or refer to the latest HMRC guidelines for personalized advice.
