**Role:** Software Developer (3–6 years) | **Stack:** Java / Spring Boot  
**Format:** In-person, ~1 hour | **Focus:** Cultural fit, ways of working, light tech discussion

---

## Important Reminders Before You Go In

- Give **sufficient detail** to convey the complexity of projects you've worked on
- Highlight your **collaborative nature** and teamwork
- Be **reflective** — show personal growth and self-awareness
- Talk about your impact, but **stay humble** — say "we" more than "I"
- **Quantify** where possible — e.g. "reduced response time by 40%" beats "improved performance significantly"
- Avoid cliché answers — especially "my weakness is perfectionism"

## Government Digital Service (GDS) Standards

All public sector digital services in the UK must meet the GDS 14-point standard, which mandates user-centric design, accessibility, and secure technology. Fivium builds products following the **GDS agile delivery phases**:

1. **Discovery** — Research the problem and determine whether a digital service is needed. No code is written.
2. **Alpha** — Build prototypes to test the most difficult technical and user assumptions.
3. **Beta** — Build a working version, release to a restricted group (private beta), then open more widely (public beta).
4. **Live** — Maintain and continuously improve based on real user data.

Backend code must pass **formal GDS service assessments** at each gate — this is a different discipline to purely commercial software development. Knowing this framework shows you understand the environment.

### "Give an example of a time you handled conflict."

**Use STAR format. Ideal framing: a technical disagreement, not interpersonal drama.**

- A disagreement over architecture, library choice, or how to approach a complex backend problem
- Show you listened fully before presenting your view
- Show the outcome was reached collaboratively, not by one person winning
- Ideally: either you changed your mind (shows humility) or persuaded with evidence (shows confidence)
- End with what you or the team learned

> *Structure: "There was a situation where [colleague/team] had a different view on [technical decision]. I made sure to listen to their reasoning fully before sharing mine. We ended up [discussed tradeoffs / took it to a design review / wrote up the options]. What we landed on was [outcome], and what I took from it was [reflection]."*

---

### "Give an example of a challenge you faced and how you overcame it."

**Use STAR format. Strongest angle for your background: a complex API/integration problem.**

- **Set the complexity clearly upfront** — don't assume they'll infer it
- Show you diagnosed methodically, not just hacked a fix
- Mention collaboration — who did you loop in, who did you lean on?
- Quantify the result if possible (e.g. "processing time dropped from 8 minutes to under 30 seconds")
- End with a reflection: "What I learned from that was..."

**Good scenario shapes to draw from:**
1. Integration between two systems that had unexpected complexity (schema mismatch, auth issues, unreliable upstream, data volume)
2. A legacy codebase where you had to make changes safely without full test coverage
3. A project where requirements changed significantly mid-delivery and you had to adapt
4. **A time you mentored or upskilled a junior developer** — this is a core part of the role and having a specific example here will stand out
