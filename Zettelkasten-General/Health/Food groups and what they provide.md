1. **Carbohydrates**
    - **Provides:** Energy in the form of glucose. Found in grains, fruits, and vegetables.
    - **Examples:** Whole grains (brown rice, whole wheat bread, oats), starchy vegetables (potatoes, corn), legumes (lentils, beans), and fruits (bananas, apples).
    - **Energy Effect:**
        - **Short-term energy**: Simple carbs (white bread, candy, soda) cause quick energy spikes and crashes.
        - **Long-term energy**: Complex carbs (whole grains, legumes) provide steady energy release.
2. **Proteins**
    - **Provides:** Amino acids for muscle repair, enzyme function, and immune support.
    - **Examples:** Lean meats (chicken, turkey, beef), fish (salmon, tuna), eggs, dairy (cheese, yogurt), plant-based sources (tofu, beans, nuts, quinoa).
    - **Energy Effect:** Provides **long-term energy** when carbohydrates are low. Helps maintain muscle mass and satiety.
3. **Fats**
    - **Provides:** Essential fatty acids, insulation, and long-lasting energy.
    - **Examples:** Healthy oils (olive oil, coconut oil), nuts and seeds (almonds, walnuts, chia seeds), fatty fish (salmon, mackerel), avocados, butter.
    - **Energy Effect:** Provides **long-term energy** that is slow and sustained. Supports brain function and overall health.
4. **Vitamins & Minerals**
    - **Provides:** Micronutrients that support metabolism, immunity, and overall body functions.
    - **Examples:**
        - **Vitamins:** Fruits (oranges for vitamin C, carrots for vitamin A), leafy greens (spinach for vitamin K), dairy (milk for vitamin D).
        - **Minerals:** Red meat (iron), bananas (potassium), nuts (magnesium), seafood (iodine, zinc).
    - **Energy Effect:** Does not provide direct energy but helps optimize metabolism and prevent fatigue.
5. **Dairy (Calcium-Rich Foods)**
    - **Provides:** Calcium, vitamin D, and protein for strong bones and muscle function.
    - **Examples:** Milk, cheese, yogurt, fortified plant-based alternatives (almond milk, soy milk).
    - **Energy Effect:** Contributes to **long-term energy** by supporting muscle and nerve function.
6. **Water**
    - **Provides:** Hydration, temperature regulation, and digestion support.
    - **Examples:** Plain water, herbal teas, water-rich fruits and vegetables (cucumbers, watermelon, celery).
    - **Energy Effect:** Does not provide calories but is **essential for sustained energy** by aiding nutrient transport and preventing fatigue.

This breakdown ensures a well-balanced diet that supports both immediate and long-lasting energy needs.
