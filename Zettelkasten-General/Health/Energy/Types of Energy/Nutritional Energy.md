* Nutritional energy refers to the **chemical energy** stored in food.
* The body converts this into **ATP (adenosine triphosphate)**, the molecule that fuels cellular processes.
* Energy from food is measured in **kilocalories (kcal)** or **kilojoules (kJ)**.

---

# Macronutrients: Primary Energy Sources

| Macronutrient     | Energy Yield | Main Use                  | Notes                                                                      |
| ----------------- | ------------ | ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Carbohydrates** | 4 kcal/g     | Fast energy               | Main energy source for brain and muscles. Stored as glycogen.              |
| **Fats**          | 9 kcal/g     | Long-term energy storage  | Slow-burning energy. Essential for hormones, cell membranes.               |
| **Proteins**      | 4 kcal/g     | Structural/repair, backup | Not primary for energy unless carbs/fats are low. Broken into amino acids. |
| **Alcohol**       | 7 kcal/g     | Non-essential energy      | Metabolized in liver. Not stored or preferred as energy.                   |

---

# Carbohydrates: Quick vs Sustained Energy

**Simple Carbs** (e.g. sugar, white bread)

* Rapid digestion → quick blood glucose spike → short energy burst
* Causes insulin spike, often followed by crash
* Good for: immediate energy (e.g. during sports)

**Complex Carbs** (e.g. oats, brown rice, legumes)

* Slower digestion → stable glucose release → sustained energy
* Higher in fiber → better blood sugar control
* Good for: long-term focus and endurance

---

# Fats: Long-Term, Stable Energy

**Unsaturated Fats** (olive oil, nuts, seeds, avocado)

* Support brain function, hormone production
* Provide stable energy during fasting or low-carb states

**Saturated Fats** (animal fat, dairy, coconut)

* Also used for energy, but excessive intake affects cardiovascular health

**Stored Fat**

* Body’s long-term energy reserve
* Burned when glucose is low (e.g. between meals, overnight, fasting)

---

# Protein: Backup and Recovery Fuel

* Not designed as a primary energy source
* Used for energy in prolonged fasting or heavy endurance activity
* High thermic effect (burns more calories to digest)

---

# Short-Term vs Long-Term Energy

| Duration        | Primary Source             | Characteristics                                   | Examples                                |
| --------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Short-Term**  | Glucose (simple carbs)     | Fast digestion, quick ATP production, short-lived | Fruit juice, white bread, energy drinks |
| **Medium-Term** | Glycogen, complex carbs    | Stored in liver/muscles, released over hours      | Oats, sweet potatoes, whole grains      |
| **Long-Term**   | Fat stores, dietary fats   | Slow digestion, used in low-insulin states        | Nuts, fatty fish, fasting between meals |
| **Backup**      | Protein (muscle breakdown) | Used in starvation or extreme exercise            | Not ideal, breaks down lean tissue      |

---

# Timing and Combinations

**Best energy stability**:

* Combine carbs, protein, and fat in meals
* Example: Chicken + brown rice + avocado → balance of fast, medium, and slow energy

**Pre-workout**:

* Emphasize simple/moderate carbs for quick energy

**Post-workout**:

* Combine carbs and protein to replenish glycogen and support muscle repair

**Before bed**:

* Include fat and protein to support overnight recovery and stable blood sugar

---

# Practical Summary

| Food Group       | Energy Role                          | Best Used When                    |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| Fruit            | Quick burst of energy                | Pre-workout, energy dips          |
| Whole grains     | Sustained energy                     | Throughout day                    |
| Nuts/seeds       | Long-term energy, stable release     | Snacks, long gaps between meals   |
| Lean protein     | Supports recovery, satiety           | All meals                         |
| Leafy vegetables | Low energy, high micronutrient value | Add to meals to balance digestion |

---

# Final Points

* Your body cycles between using **glucose and fat** based on activity, time since last meal, and insulin levels.
* Energy crashes often result from **relying too heavily on simple carbs** without fat/protein.
* Stable energy comes from **meal composition**, **timing**, and **sleep/stress management**.
