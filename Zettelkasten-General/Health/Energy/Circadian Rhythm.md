* It’s your **body’s internal clock**, primarily governed by the **suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN)** in the brain’s hypothalamus.
* The SCN uses **light signals (especially blue light)** from the eyes to align your biological processes with the 24-hour day.
* It influences:

  * **Sleep-wake cycles**
  * **Hormone release**
  * **Body temperature**
  * **Alertness and focus**
  * **Digestion and metabolism**

---

# Key Components of the Circadian System

| Component                 | Function                                                                               |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Light exposure**        | Main synchronizer (zeitgeber). Direct sunlight affects melatonin and cortisol release. |
| **Melatonin**             | Hormone released in darkness; promotes sleep.                                          |
| **Cortisol**              | Rises in the morning to wake you up; dips at night.                                    |
| **Core body temperature** | Rises during the day, drops at night. Impacts energy and alertness.                    |
| **Peripheral clocks**     | Present in organs like liver, gut. Synced by eating times.                             |

# Typical Daily Circadian Rhythm Timeline (Assumes \~7–8 a.m. Wake Time)

| Time               | Physiological State                                           |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **6–8 a.m.**       | Cortisol peaks → wake up                                      |
| **8–10 a.m.**      | Gradual alertness increase                                    |
| **10 a.m.–2 p.m.** | Peak alertness, focus, physical performance                   |
| **2–4 p.m.**       | Natural dip (body temp drops slightly)                        |
| **4–6 p.m.**       | Secondary performance peak                                    |
| **6–9 p.m.**       | Melatonin suppression, gradual wind-down                      |
| **9–10 p.m.**      | Melatonin rises → sleep pressure builds                       |
| **10 p.m.–2 a.m.** | Deep sleep window → physical recovery, growth hormone release |
| **2–5 a.m.**       | REM-dominant sleep → memory consolidation, dreaming           |
| **5–6 a.m.**       | Core temp starts rising → prepares for waking                 |

# Energy and Alertness Across the Day

* **Peak Alertness**: \~10 a.m.–2 p.m.

  * High body temp, dopamine/norepinephrine support focus and drive
* **Afternoon Dip**: \~2–4 p.m.

  * **Not laziness**: it's a built-in rest signal
  * Mild drop in body temp and alertness
* **Evening Wind-down**:

  * Light fading triggers melatonin
  * Reduced cortisol = lower energy output
* **Nighttime**:

  * Deep sleep supports **physical repair**
  * REM sleep supports **cognitive and emotional repair**

# Light’s Role

| Light Type                    | Effect                                              |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Morning light**             | Anchors rhythm, reduces melatonin, boosts serotonin |
| **Daylight**                  | Enhances mood, maintains cortisol curve             |
| **Blue light at night**       | Delays melatonin, shifts rhythm later               |
| **Red/orange light at night** | Less impact on melatonin                            |

---

# Eating and Circadian Rhythm

* **Food timing** also entrains circadian clocks in the gut, liver, and pancreas.
* Irregular eating disrupts metabolic rhythms.
* Eating late (e.g., after 9 p.m.):

  * Impairs glucose tolerance
  * May delay sleep onset
* Best energy comes from aligning meals with natural rhythms:

  * **Protein/fat-heavy breakfast** → alertness
  * **Carb-heavy dinner** → supports serotonin and sleep

# Disruption Effects

| Disruptor               | Impact                                    |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Night shifts / jet lag  | Cortisol-melatonin misalignment, fatigue  |
| Blue light at night     | Insomnia, delayed sleep onset             |
| Sleeping in on weekends | Social jet lag → Monday fatigue           |
| Inconsistent meals      | Poor digestion, low metabolic flexibility |

# Practical Alignment Tips

| Habit                               | Reason                                 |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **Wake up at same time daily**      | Anchors cortisol-melatonin cycle       |
| **Get sunlight within 30 min**      | Suppresses melatonin, boosts alertness |
| **Avoid caffeine after 2 p.m.**     | Prevents circadian delay               |
| **Eat meals at regular times**      | Keeps metabolic rhythm stable          |
| **Dim lights after 8 p.m.**         | Allows melatonin to rise               |
| **Avoid screens 1 hour before bed** | Reduces melatonin suppression          |

# Summary

* Circadian rhythm is a **light-entrained system** that regulates alertness, sleep, and metabolism.
* Your **energy highs and lows are predictable** and **not always caused by behavior**—they’re partly biological.
* Aligning with your circadian rhythm improves **energy stability, sleep quality, and cognitive performance**.
