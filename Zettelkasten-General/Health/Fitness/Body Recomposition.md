# What it is
Body recomposition is the process of simultaneously reducing body fat and increasing lean muscle mass. Unlike traditional dieting, which focuses on weight loss, or bulking, which prioritizes muscle gain often with fat gain, recomposition changes what your body is made of, not just how much it weighs.

## Why weight isn’t the focus
- The scale doesn’t distinguish between fat, muscle, water, or glycogen.
- Two people at the **same weight** can look **completely different depending on their muscle-to-fat ratio**.
- In recomposition, your weight may stay the same or even increase slightly, while your physique, strength, and metabolic health improve.

## Why body recomposition is beneficial
- **Aesthetic improvements**: More muscle, less fat, better shape and definition without extreme swings.
- Health improvements: Lower visceral fat reduces risk of metabolic disease, while more muscle improves insulin sensitivity, bone density, and mobility.
- **Performance**: Muscle strength and endurance improve without having to accept fat gain.
- **Sustainability**: Avoids the mental and physical strain of repeated bulking/cutting cycles

# How to do body recomposition
1. Training
    - Strength training 3–5 times per week with progressive overload.
    - Focus on compound lifts (squats, deadlifts, presses, rows, pull-ups).
    - Some cardio is fine but shouldn’t interfere with lifting recovery.
2. Nutrition
    - Protein: For body recomposition (gaining muscle and losing fat), your protein intake should be significantly higher than the standard recommendation.
	    - Based on your weight of 65.4 kg, you should target an intake in the range of **105 grams to 165 grams of protein per day.**
    - Calories: Around maintenance or in a slight deficit. Too low = muscle loss, too high = fat gain.
    - Food quality: Emphasize whole foods, fiber, and micronutrients.
3. Recovery
    - Sleep 7–9 hours per night.
    - Manage stress to control cortisol (which can interfere with both fat loss and muscle growth).
4. Tracking
    - Don’t rely only on the scale.
    - Track: strength progress, waist/hip/arm measurements, progress photos, and body fat percentage if possible.

# Why not just cut then bulk?
Cutting → you lose fat but risk losing muscle.
Bulking → you gain muscle but also fat.
Cycle repeats → often leads to weight yo-yoing and frustration.

Recomposition avoids those swings, but it requires patience and consistency. Bulking/cutting can still be useful if you’re advanced, very lean, or chasing maximum gains in the shortest time.

# Summary
In short: Body recomposition is the process of changing what your body is made of — less fat, more muscle — without focusing on just the number on the scale. It works best for beginners, intermediates, and anyone who values sustainability over speed.
