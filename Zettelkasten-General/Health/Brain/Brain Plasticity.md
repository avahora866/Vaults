Brain plasticity, also known as neuroplasticity, is the brain's ability to change and adapt throughout life. This includes forming new neural connections, reorganizing existing pathways, and even compensating for injury or damage. Neuroplasticity plays a key role in learning, memory, recovery from brain injuries, and adapting to new experiences.

There are two main types of neuroplasticity:

1. Structural Plasticity – The brain physically changes its structure in response to learning or experience
2. Functional Plasticity – The brain shifts functions from damaged areas to undamaged ones after injury.

This adaptability allows humans to develop new skills, recover from strokes, and adjust to environmental changes.
