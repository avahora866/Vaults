# Three main parts of the brain
- Cerebrum: The largest part, located at the front and top of your skull.
- Cerebellum: Situated at the back, beneath the cerebrum.
- Brainstem: Connects the cerebrum and cerebellum to the spinal cord.

## Cerebrum
The cerebrum is the largest part of the brain and is divided into two halves:
- Left hemisphere
- Right hemisphere
Both hemispheres communicate with each other through a thick band of nerve fibers called the corpus callosum. The cerebrum responsible for all your high-level thinking.

The left and right hemisphere are further split into 4 lobes:
- Frontal Lobe: Located behind your forehead, this is your personality and decision-making hub. It handles problem-solving, planning, and voluntary movements.
- Parietal Lobe: Found at the top and back of your head, it processes sensory information like touch, temperature, and pain. It's also involved in navigation and spatial awareness.
- Temporal Lobe: Situated on the sides of your head, near your ears. This lobe is key for processing sounds, understanding language, and creating memories.
- Occipital Lobe: At the very back of the brain, this is the visual processing center. It takes in information from your eyes and interprets it, allowing you to see.

## Cerebellum
The cerebellum, which means "little brain" in Latin, is crucial for coordination and balance. It fine-tunes your motor movements, making them smooth and precise rather than clumsy and jerky.

## Brainstem
The brainstem is the oldest and most basic part of the brain. It connects the brain to the spinal cord and manages all the vital functions that keep you alive without you ever having to think about them. This includes:
- Breathing
- Heart rate
- Swallowing
- Blood pressure
- Sleeping and waking

# Brain Cells
The brain is made up of billions of specialized cells called neurons. These cells communicate with each other using tiny electrical and chemical signals. They form a vast, interconnected network that sends messages throughout your brain and body at incredible speeds.
