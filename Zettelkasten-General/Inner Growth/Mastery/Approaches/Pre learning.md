Pre-learning refers to the process of acquiring foundational knowledge or skills before engaging in a new learning experience. It prepares the brain for deeper understanding by activating prior knowledge, creating mental frameworks, and reducing cognitive load when encountering new information.

# How Pre-Learning Works

1. Activating Prior Knowledge – The brain connects new information to what it already knows, making learning more efficient.
2. Priming Cognitive Structures – Exposure to key concepts beforehand helps learners recognize patterns and absorb information more easily.
3. Reducing Cognitive Load – When learners already have some familiarity with a topic, they can focus on deeper understanding rather than struggling with basic concepts.

# Examples of Pre-Learning

- Reading a summary of a complex book before diving into the full text.
- Watching an introductory video before attending a lecture.
- Reviewing vocabulary words before learning a new language concept.
