**Expected Utility Theory (EUT)** helps individuals make rational decisions under uncertainty by weighing the **probabilities** and **utilities** (subjective values) of outcomes. The individual chooses the option with the **highest expected utility**.

# Key Components (Personal Examples)

| Component            | Description                                              | Example                                                |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Alternatives**     | The options you're choosing between                      | Take a new job, stay in current job, go back to school |
| **Outcomes**         | Possible results of each alternative                     | Higher salary, same salary, no job after school        |
| **Probabilities**    | Likelihood of each outcome                               | 60% chance of better pay, 30% same, 10% worse          |
| **Utilities**        | Personal value of each outcome                           | High salary = 90, same salary = 50, no job = 10        |
| **Expected Utility** | Weighted sum of utilities based on outcome probabilities | EU = (0.6×90) + (0.3×50) + (0.1×10) = 70 units         |

---

# Steps to Apply EUT (Individual Context)
1. **Define Alternatives**: E.g. move to a new city, stay in place, or accept a remote role.
2. **List Possible Outcomes**: For each option: success, partial success, failure (e.g., job satisfaction, pay, stress levels).
3. **Assign Probabilities**: Estimate how likely each outcome is for each alternative.
4. **Assign Utilities**: Based on how much each outcome matters to you (not just money — could be stress, free time, etc.).
5. **Calculate Expected Utilities**: Use the formula:

   $$
   EU = \sum (P_i \times U_i)
   $$

6. **Pick the Option with Highest EU**

# Example: Choosing Between Job Offers

| Outcome               | Probability | Utility | Weighted Value |
| --------------------- | ----------- | ------- | -------------- |
| High pay, low stress  | 0.5         | 90      | 45             |
| High pay, high stress | 0.3         | 60      | 18             |
| Job dissatisfaction   | 0.2         | 20      | 4              |
| **Expected Utility**  |             |         | **67**         |

Compare this EU to staying at your current job or taking another offer.
