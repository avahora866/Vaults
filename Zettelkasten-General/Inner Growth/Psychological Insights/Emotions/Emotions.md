# 6 basic emotions

Psychologist Paul Ekman identified six basic emotions that are universally recognized across cultures:

1. [[Happiness|Happiness (Joy)]]:A feeling of pleasure, contentment, or satisfaction, often accompanied by smiling or laughter.
2. Sadness: A feeling of sorrow, loss, or disappointment, often leading to tears or withdrawal from others.
3. Fear: A reaction to danger or the threat of harm, triggering a fight-or-flight response. Fear causes increased heart rate, tension, and vigilance.
4. [[Anger]]: A strong feeling of displeasure or hostility in response to a perceived wrong or injustice. It can result in frustration, irritability, or aggression.
5. Surprise: A brief emotional reaction to something unexpected, often neutral, but can be positive (pleasant surprise) or negative (shock).
6. Disgust: A feeling of strong aversion or revulsion, often towards something perceived as offensive, unpleasant, or harmful.

# Complex Emotions:

These emotions are often combinations of basic emotions and can be influenced by personal experiences, culture, and social factors.

1. Love: A deep emotional connection or affection for someone or something, which can encompass caring, attachment, and passion.
2. Embarrassment: A feeling of self-consciousness, discomfort, or awkwardness, typically in response to a minor social mistake or public attention.
3. Shame: A more profound feeling of inadequacy or guilt, often connected to a sense of personal failure, and leading to a desire to hide or withdraw.
4. Guilt: A sense of regret or responsibility for a wrong action, often linked to a desire to make amends or correct the wrongdoing.
5. Pride: A feeling of satisfaction and accomplishment, often related to personal achievements, values, or group affiliations. It can have positive or negative connotations (e.g., healthy pride vs. arrogance).
6. Jealousy: A complex emotion involving feelings of insecurity, fear, and resentment, typically in response to a perceived threat to a relationship or one’s success.
7. Envy: A feeling of longing for someone else’s advantages, possessions, or qualities. Unlike jealousy, it doesn't involve a perceived threat but rather a desire for what others have.
8. Relief: A sense of relaxation or release from tension, often experienced after a stressful or unpleasant situation has ended.
9. Anxiety: A state of worry, nervousness, or unease, often about uncertain or future events. Anxiety can involve both emotional and physical reactions.
10. Anticipation: A feeling of excitement or dread when expecting something, often combined with hope or fear for the future.
11. Contentment: A quiet, peaceful sense of satisfaction and well-being, often tied to accepting life as it is.
12. Loneliness: A feeling of sadness or isolation due to a lack of companionship or meaningful social connection.
13. Gratitude: A feeling of thankfulness or appreciation, often directed toward others for their kindness or generosity.
14. Frustration: A feeling of irritation and impatience when something is blocked or thwarted, often accompanied by anger or helplessness.
15. Hope: A sense of optimism or expectation for a positive outcome in the future, often tied to a belief in possibilities or change.
16. Despair: A profound feeling of hopelessness and helplessness, often experienced when one feels unable to improve a situation.
17. Awe: A feeling of wonder and amazement, often experienced in the presence of something vast, powerful, or extraordinary.
18. Confusion: A state of uncertainty or puzzlement, often involving conflicting thoughts or a lack of understanding.
19. Compassion: A deep feeling of sympathy and concern for the suffering or misfortune of others, often accompanied by a desire to help.
20. Nostalgia :A wistful or sentimental longing for the past, often idealized as better than the present.
21. Boredom: A feeling of restlessness or dissatisfaction when one is uninterested or unstimulated by their current situation or surroundings.

# Other Emotional States:

Some emotions are a bit harder to categorize, but they are often a mix of the basic emotions:

1. Curiosity: A desire to learn or know more about something, often driven by interest and wonder.
2. Regret: A sense of sadness or remorse about a past action or decision.
3. Empathy: The ability to understand and share the feelings of another person, often leading to compassion or kindness.

These are just a sample of the emotions humans experience. Emotions are fluid and can vary in intensity, often overlapping or occurring together.
