From a cognitive psychology standpoint, **context switching** refers to the mental process of shifting attention and cognitive resources from one task or set of information to another. It involves moving between different "cognitive contexts," or ways of thinking, which may require different cognitive processes (e.g., problem-solving, memory retrieval, attention control). This is commonly referred to as **task switching**.

When we switch between tasks, especially in complex or unrelated activities, our brain needs to disengage from the current task, redirect attention, and reorient itself to the new task. Here’s how it works:

1. **Attention Shift**: The brain has limited cognitive resources (e.g., working memory, attention). When you switch tasks, you have to disengage from the current task and reallocate resources to the new one.
2. **Set Shifting**: You move from one mental "set" to another. For instance, when answering an email (which requires written communication) and then switching to solving a math problem (which requires logical reasoning), your brain needs to adjust its mental approach.
3. **Cognitive Load**: Every time you switch, it increases cognitive load. This is because you may need to recall the context of the previous task, discard irrelevant information, and reorient yourself. This can lead to increased mental fatigue and slower performance, especially if the tasks are unrelated.
4. **Time Cost**: Each switch can introduce a **time cost**, often called the **switching cost**. Studies show that it can take time for your brain to "warm up" to the new task and get back to optimal performance after the switch. This is why multitasking or constantly switching tasks can often lead to inefficiency.
5. **Performance Degradation**: While people believe they can multitask efficiently, frequent context switching tends to reduce performance. This happens because the brain isn't as efficient when it constantly needs to refocus and reorganize cognitive resources. It can lead to errors, slower task completion, or difficulty in retaining information.

In essence, frequent context switching reduces efficiency due to the cognitive overhead involved in transitioning between tasks, especially if the tasks require different cognitive skills or processes.
