"duration-based" versus "completion-based."

A task that requires doing something for 10 minutes is duration-based (it depends on time spent).

A task that requires completing an action is completion-based (it depends on finishing a specific goal).
# When to Use Duration-Based vs. Completion-Based Tasks

## Use Duration-Based Tasks When:

1. Building Habits – Encourages consistency without overwhelming effort (e.g., "Read for 20 minutes").
2. Improving Endurance or Focus – Useful for practice, studying, or workouts (e.g., "Write for 30 minutes").
3. Avoiding Perfectionism – Helps people start tasks without stressing about completion (e.g., "Draw for 15 minutes").
4. Time-Limited Situations – When you only have a set amount of time (e.g., "Practice guitar for 10 minutes before work").


## Use Completion-Based Tasks When:
1. Achieving Specific Goals – Ensures a concrete result (e.g., "Write 500 words").
2. Task-Oriented Work – Best for checklists and projects with clear deliverables (e.g., "Send three emails").
3. Tracking Progress by Milestones – Useful when measuring improvement (e.g., "Solve 10 math problems").
4. Maximizing Productivity – Encourages efficiency and goal completion rather than just effort (e.g., "Finish coding this feature").

# Why I avoid tasks
- Low Expected Value
    - Your brain calculates rewards vs. effort (Expected Utility Theory).
- Ego Depletion / Fatigue
    - If you've used willpower earlier in the day, you’re less likely to engage hard tasks later.
- Task Aversion via Negative Emotion
    - Your brain links certain tasks with discomfort:
        - Anxiety (e.g. making a call)
        - Shame (e.g. undone chores)
        - Boredom (e.g. repetitive work)
    - These emotions act as avoidance triggers.
- Goal Conflicts
    - You may subconsciously value another outcome more:
        - Task: “Write job application”
        - Conflict: “I want to avoid rejection” → avoid task.
    - Internal friction leads to procrastination.
- Temporal Discounting
    - Your brain undervalues distant rewards.
    - If the benefit is delayed, motivation drops sharply.
    - Future You gets stuck with the job.
- Lack of Identity Alignment
    - Your brain undervalues distant rewards.
        - If the benefit is delayed, motivation drops sharply.
        - Future You gets stuck with the job.
