If you understand something, you tend to place more importance on it than on things you don’t understand. This can be harmful because you might make decisions based only on what you know, ignoring what you don’t know. Ideally, you would fully understand a topic and then focus only on the priorities within it. Since knowing everything is impossible, the best approach is to at least be aware of your knowledge gaps. When making decisions, reflect on what you don’t know and consider researching those areas if they might affect your choice.

---

# Key points on managing knowledge and decision-making:
* **Understanding increases perceived importance:** You value what you know more than what you don’t.
* **Risk of bias:** Acting only on known information can overlook critical unknowns.
* **Ideal but unrealistic:** Knowing everything before deciding isn’t practical.
* **Awareness of gaps:** Recognize what you don’t know as part of decision-making.
* **Reflect and research:** Use awareness of unknowns to guide further investigation when needed.

---

In short, avoid overvaluing what you know by consciously accounting for what you don’t know. This improves decision quality by balancing confidence in known facts with curiosity about unknowns.
