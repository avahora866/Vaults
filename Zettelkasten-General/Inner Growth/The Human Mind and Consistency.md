The human mind demands consistency because **inconsistency is metabolically expensive and dangerous.**

Your brain is not designed for "truth"—it is designed for **efficiency** and **survival**.

The drive for internal consistency comes down to three biological imperatives:
* **Predictive Processing (Energy Conservation):**
    Your brain is a prediction machine. It constantly builds a model of the world to guess what will happen next.
    * **Consistency:** When your beliefs match reality, your brain burns very little energy. It runs on autopilot.
    * **Inconsistency:** When you hold conflicting beliefs (or reality contradicts your belief), your brain registers a "prediction error." This triggers a spike in neural activity to resolve the conflict. It is physically exhausting. Your brain fights this to conserve glucose and energy.
* **Actionable Certainty (Survival):**
    In an evolutionary context, hesitation is fatal.
    * If you believe "that noise is the wind" AND "that noise is a tiger," you freeze.
    * To act (run or relax), you must collapse those two possibilities into one consistent reality. The brain forces a choice—even a wrong one—to enable movement.
* **Social Trust (Tribe Cohesion):**
    Humans are social animals. If your behavior is erratic (inconsistent with your words or past actions), the tribe cannot predict you. Unpredictable members are dangerous and often ostracized. We crave consistency to signal to others: *"I am safe. You can trust me."*
