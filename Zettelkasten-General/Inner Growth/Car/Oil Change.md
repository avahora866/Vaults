Oil changes are essential maintenance for your car's engine. Here is a breakdown of why you need them, what the oil does, and the difference between changing and adding oil.

### Why You Need an Oil Change

You need to change your car's oil because it degrades and becomes contaminated over time, which reduces its ability to protect the engine.

* **Oil degrades:** Engine oil has additives that improve its performance (like detergents and anti-wear agents). Over time, heat and stress break down these additives, making the oil less effective.
* **Contaminants build up:** As oil circulates, it collects dirt, microscopic metal shavings from engine wear, and by-products of combustion (like water and acids). This turns the oil dark, thick, and gritty, forming **sludge**.
* **Reduced effectiveness:** Dirty, degraded oil cannot lubricate, cool, or clean the engine components properly. This increases friction and heat, leading to premature wear and potentially costly engine damage.

Regularly replacing the old, dirty oil with new oil and a new filter maintains engine efficiency and extends the engine's life.

### What Engine Oil Actually Does

Engine oil has four primary functions in a car:

1.  **Lubrication:** It creates a thin, protective film between the many fast-moving metal parts (like pistons, valves, and bearings) to prevent them from grinding against each other. This is its most critical function.
2.  **Cooling:** It absorbs heat from the hottest parts of the engine, like the combustion chambers and pistons, and carries it away to the oil pan where it dissipates.
3.  **Cleaning:** It contains detergents that suspend dirt, wear particles, and sludge, keeping them from sticking to the engine's internal surfaces. The oil filter traps these contaminants.
4.  **Protection:** It contains corrosion inhibitors that form a protective layer on metal surfaces to guard against rust and acid damage, which are by-products of combustion.

### Changing Oil vs. Filling Up Oil (Topping Up)

The terms **changing oil** and **filling up/topping up oil** refer to two very different maintenance tasks.

| Feature | Changing Oil (Oil Change) | Filling Up Oil (Topping Up) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Purpose** | To replace **all** the old, degraded, and contaminated oil with new, clean oil. | To add a small amount of new oil to raise the level when it falls below the minimum safe mark. |
| **Oil Status** | All old oil is drained out. | New oil is mixed with the existing old, used oil. |
| **Oil Filter** | The old oil filter is **always** replaced to remove trapped contaminants. | The oil filter is **not** replaced. |
| **Frequency** | Based on the manufacturer's recommended mileage or time interval (e.g., every 8,000 kilometres or 6 months), whichever comes first. | As needed, typically when the oil level is checked and found to be low. |
| **Effectiveness** | Full engine protection is restored with fresh lubricant and a clean filter. | Only fixes a low oil level; it does not remove contaminants or restore the full protective properties of the old oil. |

**Key Takeaway:**

* **Topping up** is an emergency measure or a routine correction for low oil level, which can occur between changes due to a small amount of oil being consumed by the engine. It ensures the engine has enough oil to avoid catastrophic failure from friction.
* **Changing the oil** is the complete removal of the old, dirty, and chemically broken-down oil and filter. You must change your oil and filter regularly because simply adding new oil to old, dirty oil will not protect your engine effectively.
