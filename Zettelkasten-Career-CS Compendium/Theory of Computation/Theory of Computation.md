The field Theory of Compution of the subjects theoretical computer science and mathmatics deals with:
- what problems can be solved on a model of computation using an algorithm
- how efficiently they can be solved
- to what degree they can be solved

There are three major branches of this field

- [[Automata Theory]]: Automata theory is the study of abstract machines (or more appropriately, abstract 'mathematical' machines or systems) and the computational problems that can be solved using these machines. These abstract machines are called automata
- [[Computability Theory]]: Computability theory deals primarily with the question of the extent to which a problem is solvable on a computer
- [[Computational complexity]]: Computational complexity theory considers not only whether a problem can be solved at all on a computer, but also how efficiently the problem can be solved. Two major aspects are considered: time complexity and space complexity, which are respectively how many steps it takes to perform a computation, and how much memory is required to perform that computation
