Trigonometry is a branch of mathematics that studies the relationships between the angles and sides of triangles, particularly right-angled triangles. It involves several key concepts and tools:

# Similar Triangles

Similar triangles have the same shape but may differ in size. Their corresponding angles are equal, and the ratios of their corresponding sides are proportional. This property is fundamental to trigonometry, as it allows the definition of trigonometric ratios that are consistent for a given angle, regardless of the triangle's size.

# Soh Cah Toa
This mnemonic helps remember the basic trigonometric ratios for right-angled triangles:

- **Soh**: Sine = Opposite / Hypotenuse
- **Cah**: Cosine = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
- **Toa**: Tangent = Opposite / Adjacent

# Sine and Cosine Law
#NOTE 
These laws generalize the basic trigonometric ratios to non-right-angled triangles:

- **Sine Law**: a/sin(A) = b/sin(B) = c/sin(C), useful for finding unknown angles or sides when given two angles and one side or two sides and a non-included angle.
- **Cosine Law**: c² = a² + b² - 2ab * cos(C), useful for finding an unknown side or angle when given two sides and the included angle or all three sides.

# Special Triangles
#NOTE 
Two commonly used special triangles are:

- **45°-45°-90° Triangle**: The sides are in the ratio 1:1:√2.
- **30°-60°-90° Triangle**: The sides are in the ratio 1:√3:2.  
    These triangles simplify calculations for common angles.

# Unit Circle and CAST Rule
#NOTE 
The **unit circle** is a circle with a radius of 1 centered at the origin of the coordinate plane. It is fundamental for defining trigonometric functions for all angles. In the unit circle:

- Sine is the y-coordinate.
- Cosine is the x-coordinate.
- Tangent is the ratio of y/x.  
    The **CAST rule** helps remember the sign of trigonometric functions in each quadrant:
- Quadrant I: All positive
- Quadrant II: Sine positive
- Quadrant III: Tangent positive
- Quadrant IV: Cosine positive

# Ratios for Angles Greater Than 90 Degrees
#NOTE 
Trigonometric functions are extended to angles greater than 90 degrees using the unit circle. For example, sin(θ) and cos(θ) are periodic, repeating every 360° (or 2π radians), while tan(θ) repeats every 180° (or π radians).

# Sine and Cosine Functions
#NOTE 
These are periodic functions:

- **Sine function**: y = sin(θ) is periodic with a period of 2π and ranges from -1 to 1.
- **Cosine function**: y = cos(θ) also has a period of 2π and ranges from -1 to 1.  
    They are useful for modeling waves and oscillations.

# Radians
#NOTE 
Radians are an alternative unit for measuring angles, where one complete revolution around the unit circle is 2π radians. The conversion is:

- 180° = π radians

# Trigonometric Identities
#NOTE 
These are equations involving trigonometric functions that are true for all values within their domains. Important identities include:

- **Pythagorean Identity**: sin²(θ) + cos²(θ) = 1
- **Reciprocal Identities**:
    - csc(θ) = 1/sin(θ)
    - sec(θ) = 1/cos(θ)
    - cot(θ) = 1/tan(θ)
- **Angle Sum and Difference Identities**:
    - sin(A ± B) = sin(A)cos(B) ± cos(A)sin(B)
    - cos(A ± B) = cos(A)cos(B) ∓ sin(A)sin(B)

# Solving Trigonometric Equations
#NOTE 
These involve finding the angles that satisfy a given trigonometric equation, often using identities, inverse functions, and periodicity properties. Solutions may be expressed in degrees or radians, and multiple solutions are common due to the periodic nature of trigonometric functions.

Trigonometry is widely used in various fields, including physics, engineering, computer graphics, and navigation, due to its ability to model periodic phenomena and solve geometric problems.
