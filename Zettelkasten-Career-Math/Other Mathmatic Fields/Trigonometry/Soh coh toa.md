SOH CAH TOA is a mnemonic that helps remember the three primary trigonometric ratios used in right-angled triangles:

- Sine (sin): Opposite / Hypotenuse (SOH)
- Cosine (cos): Adjacent / Hypotenuse (CAH)
- Tangent (tan): Opposite / Adjacent (TOA)

These ratios are foundational in solving right-angled triangle problems, which are widely used in fields like physics, engineering, and navigation.

In a right-angled triangle, the terms **opposite**, **adjacent**, and **hypotenuse** refer to the sides relative to a given angle (**θ**).

1. **Hypotenuse**:
    - The **longest side** of a right-angled triangle.
    - It is always **opposite** the **right angle (90°)**.
2. **Opposite Side**:
    - The side **directly across** from the angle you are considering (**θ**).
    - It does **not** touch the angle.
3. **Adjacent Side**:
    - The side **next to** the angle **θ** (excluding the hypotenuse).
    - It is the **base** of the triangle along with the hypotenuse.

### **Example for Clarity**

Imagine a right-angled triangle with an angle **θ** at the bottom left:

- The **hypotenuse** is the longest side, opposite the right angle.
- The **opposite side** is directly across from **θ**.
- The **adjacent side** is the one next to **θ**, but **not the hypotenuse**.

Generalized Problem-Solving Approach Using SOH CAH TOA

1. Identify the given values: Determine which sides of the right-angled triangle are known (opposite, adjacent, or hypotenuse).
2. Choose the appropriate ratio: Based on known and unknown values, use SOH (sine), CAH (cosine), or TOA (tangent).
3. Set up the equation: Substitute values into the trigonometric formula.
4. Solve for the unknown: Use algebraic manipulation and inverse trigonometric functions if necessary.
5. Interpret the result: Ensure the answer makes sense in the given context.

By applying these principles, SOH CAH TOA becomes a powerful tool for solving practical trigonometry problems across various disciplines.

# Examples
## Sine (SOH: Sin = Opposite / Hypotenuse)

Suppose you place a 10-foot ladder against a wall, and the base of the ladder is 4 feet away from the wall.

To find the angle (θ) between the ground and the ladder, use the sine function:

$$
\sin(\theta) = \frac{\text{opposite (height of the ladder on the wall)}}{\text{hypotenuse (length of the ladder)}}
$$
$$
\sin(\theta) = \frac{x}{10}
$$

$$
\sin(\theta) = \frac{9.17}{10} \approx 0.917
$$
## Cosine (COH: Cos = Adjacent / Hypotenuse)

A wheelchair ramp is 5 meters long (hypotenuse) and reaches a height of 1 meter (opposite side).

To find the angle (θ) of inclination, use cosine:

$$
\cos(\theta) = \frac{\text{adjacent (base of ramp)}}{\text{hypotenuse (ramp length)}}
$$
$$
\cos(\theta) = \frac{4.9}{5} \approx 0.98
$$
## Tangent (TOA: Tan = Opposite / Adjacent)

You stand 50 meters away from a building and measure the angle of elevation to the top as 30°.

To find the building’s height (h):

$$
\tan(30^\circ) = \frac{\text{opposite (height of building)}}{\text{adjacent (distance from building)}}
$$
$$
\tan(30^\circ) = \frac{h}{50} ]
$$
$$
h = 50 \times \tan(30^\circ) = 50 \times 0.577 = 28.85 \text{ meters}
$$
Each of these examples shows how SOH, COH, and TOA can be used in everyday situations!
