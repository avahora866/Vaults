#TRUNK

> Covers what happens after the opener: transitioning between topics, sustaining momentum, and closing without awkwardness.

---

## Core Concept

A conversation has three phases after it starts: **transition**, **sustain**, and **close**. These aren't always linear — but understanding each one separately is how you build the skill. The exception is the close, which is **opportunistic, not engineered**: you recognise a good exit point rather than manufacture one.

---

## Part 1 — Transitioning Between Topics

### The Core Mistake
Waiting for a topic to die before moving on. Topics exhaust slowly and painfully. **Move while there's still energy in the current thread.**

### Three Methods

| Method | How | When to Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Bridge** | Connect the new topic to something just said. *"That reminds me..."* | Default. Feels seamless. |
| **Callback** | Return to something they mentioned earlier. *"You said earlier X — what was that like?"* | When you want to signal you were listening. |
| **Declared pivot** | Name the shift openly. *"Completely different thing, but..."* | When no natural bridge exists. Cleaner than a forced one. |

---

## Part 2 — Sustaining a Conversation

### The Engine: Ask + Contribute
The balance that keeps a conversation alive. Neither alone works:

- **Only asking** → feels like an interrogation
- **Only contributing** → feels like a monologue
- **Both** → genuine exchange

### Depth vs Breadth
Know when to go deeper on the current thread versus open a new one.

- **Short answer** → they're probably not engaged with that topic. Open a branch.
- **Long answer** → they're into it. Go deeper.

### The Gift Principle
Always leave the other person something easy to respond to — a question, or a statement with an implicit invitation (*"I always find that strange"* invites *"why?"* without demanding it).

### Reading Engagement vs Politeness

| Signal | Engaged | Polite |
|---|---|---|
| Answer length | Gets longer | Stays short |
| Return questions | Yes | No |
| Volunteered info | Yes — they add things you didn't ask for | No |
| Responses feel | Open | Complete |

**Don't keep pushing a polite conversation.** It increases the awkwardness load without payoff.

---

## Part 3 — Ending Cleanly

### The Two-Part Close: Signal + Land

**1. Signal before you're ready to leave** (not as you're leaving):
> *"I should probably let you go soon"* / *"One last thing before I head off"*

This gives both people a moment to wrap mentally. Skipping it is the primary cause of abrupt endings.

**2. Land on something positive and forward-looking:**
- A callback to something good from the conversation
- A future hook: *"Let me know how that goes"* / *"We should do this again"*
- A simple, genuine close: *"This was good"*

### Timing
Exit while you're both still enjoying it. The instinct is to stay until it naturally ends — but that natural end is usually a slow decay. **Leave slightly before you feel ready.** The other person will remember the conversation as better than it was.

## Roadmap

### Phase 1 — Awareness (Weeks 1–2)
**Goal:** Stop operating on autopilot. Start noticing what's actually happening in conversations.

- [ ] After each conversation, note: did it die or did it end? What caused it?
- [ ] Identify one moment per conversation where you waited too long to transition
- [ ] Notice whether you're asking more than contributing, or the reverse
- [ ] Spot the difference between engaged and polite at least once per day

*No behaviour change yet — just observation.*

---

### Phase 2 — Transitions (Weeks 3–4)
**Goal:** Move between topics smoothly and intentionally.

- [ ] Practice the **bridge** method first — it's the most natural
- [ ] Start transitioning *before* you feel the topic is exhausted
- [ ] Use the **declared pivot** when a bridge feels forced — notice how rarely it feels jarring
- [ ] Try one **callback** per conversation — return to something they mentioned earlier

*Success signal: you're moving topics before they die, and it feels less effortful.*

---

### Phase 3 — Sustaining (Weeks 5–7)
**Goal:** Keep conversations alive without carrying them alone.

- [ ] Audit your ask/contribute ratio after conversations — which way do you lean?
- [ ] Practise the **gift principle**: end statements with something they can pick up
- [ ] When someone gives a long answer, resist opening a new topic — go deeper first
- [ ] When someone gives a short answer, pivot rather than push

*Success signal: conversations feel less like work. The other person is contributing more.*

---

### Phase 4 — Closing (Weeks 8–9)
**Goal:** End conversations cleanly and on a high.

- [ ] Practise signalling before you're ready to leave — not as you're going
- [ ] Build a personal exit phrase that feels natural to you
- [ ] Experiment with leaving slightly early — notice how it changes the other person's impression
- [ ] Always land with something forward-looking rather than a trailing silence

*Success signal: conversations end and both people feel good about them.*

---

### Phase 5 — Integration (Week 10+)
**Goal:** All three phases working together unconsciously.

- [ ] Run occasional post-conversation reviews — what phase still needs work?
- [ ] Seek out longer conversations (meals, walks) where all three phases are required
- [ ] Notice which method you default to for transitions — deliberately use the others
- [ ] Mentor or explain the framework to someone else (teaches you where gaps remain)

---

## Quick Reference

```
TRANSITION   →  Move early. Bridge → Callback → Declared pivot.
SUSTAIN      →  Ask + contribute. Short answer = branch. Long answer = deepen.
CLOSE        →  Signal first. Land on a high. Leave slightly early.
```
