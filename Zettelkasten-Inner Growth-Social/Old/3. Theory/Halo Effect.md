If you like a president’s politics, you probably also like his voice and appearance. This is an example of the _halo effect_—the tendency to judge all traits of a person based on your impression of just one.

Take someone named Joan. She’s friendly and easy to talk to. Later, her name comes up as a potential charity donor. What do you actually know about her generosity? Nothing. Being pleasant in conversation doesn’t imply she's generous.

But you like Joan, and you like generous people. So you subconsciously associate Joan with generosity. Now, believing she's generous, you might look back and like her even more—because you've added a positive trait to her image.
