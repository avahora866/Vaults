Your issue likely comes down to **context overload and inhibition**, not a fundamental inability to converse. Here's a breakdown of what may be happening:

### Possible Causes

1. **Social Comparison**
    - You may be internally comparing yourself to others in the group.
    - This can lead to overthinking or self-censorship.
2. **Turn-Taking Pressure**
    - Group conversations have faster, less predictable turn-taking.
    - You may struggle to find a gap to speak or fear interrupting.
3. **Lack of Social Role Clarity**
    - In one-on-one conversations, your role is clear.
    - In groups, you may not feel like you "belong" or have a role, so you default to silence.
4. **Cognitive Load**
    - Tracking multiple people, dynamics, and topics increases mental effort.
    - That can overwhelm your ability to think of what to say.
5. **Low Signaling**
    - You may not use enough **nonverbal cues** (eye contact, posture, lean-in) that show you're engaged and want to speak.
    - So others don’t invite you in.

### What You Can Do

- **Pre-load contributions**: Come with a few things to say about the topic beforehand.
- **Watch for entry points**: Look for when someone pauses or makes a general statement you can build on.
- **Use short interjections**: “That’s true.” “I was thinking the same.” These keep you present without needing full takes.
- **Pair with one person**: Make eye contact with one speaker and talk as if it’s just you two for that comment.
- **Lean in and nod**: Nonverbal cues help invite you into the flow.
