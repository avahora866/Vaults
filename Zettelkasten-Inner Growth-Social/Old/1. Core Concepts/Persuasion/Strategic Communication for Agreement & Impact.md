These techniques focus on guiding conversations towards agreement, avoiding arguments, handling mistakes gracefully, and using showmanship to make your message resonate.

* **Avoiding Arguments:**
    * You will never get into trouble by admitting you may be wrong. This stops arguments and inspires opponents to be fair and broad-minded, even prompting them to admit their own potential errors.
* **When You're Wrong (Self-Criticism First):**
    * It's easier to listen to self-criticism than condemnation from others. Say about yourself all the derogatory things you know the other person is thinking before they get a chance to say them.
    * *Example:* A mounted policeman, instead of scolding, told a dog walker, "You have a beautiful dog... you certainly have him well trained. I'll bet he never bites anybody. He's a beautiful dog." This approach made the dog owner willing to take responsibility and put a muzzle on his dog, even though he was initially resistant.
    * Admitting one's mistakes raises one above the common tendency to defend faults, giving a feeling of nobility.
* **The "Drop of Honey" Principle:**
    * A friendly approach is far more effective than an aggressive one.
    * *Example:* A tenant who wanted a rent reduction approached her landlord not by complaining about high rent, but by enthusiastically praising his building management. This positive reception disarmed him and led him to voluntarily offer a rent reduction, and even ask what decorating she wanted.
    * *Example:* Dean Woodcock's friendly, understanding explanation to a photographer (who was initially looking for negative publicity about his department's "waste") disarmed the photographer and turned a potential embarrassment into a friendly exchange.
* **Make Them Say "Yes" (The "Socratic Method"):**
    * Do not begin by discussing differences. Instead, emphasize and keep emphasizing points of agreement. Highlight that you are both striving for the same end, and differences are only in method, not purpose.
    * Crucially, try to keep the other person from saying "No." Once someone says "No," their pride demands consistency, making it very difficult to change their mind later, even if they realize their "No" was ill-advised.
    * A skillful speaker gets a number of "Yes" responses at the outset. When a person says "Yes," their entire being is in a forward-moving, accepting, open attitude. The more "Yeses" obtained early on, the more likely the ultimate proposal will be accepted.
* **Dramatize Your Truth (Showmanship):**
    * Merely stating a truth isn't enough; it must be made vivid, interesting, and dramatic. Use showmanship, like movies and television do, to gain attention.
    * *Example:* An old-time lover proposing on his knees dramatically showed his sincerity. Even now, creating a romantic atmosphere before proposing is a form of dramatization.
    * *Example:* Dramatizing the urgency of a meeting request helped secure an appointment quickly.
* **Stimulate Competition (Desire to Excel):**
    * To get things done, stimulate competition – not in a "money-getting way," but in the desire to excel.
    * This taps into the "game" aspect that successful people love: self-expression, proving worth, excelling, and winning. This desire is what drives foot-races, contests, and, fundamentally, the desire for a feeling of importance.
