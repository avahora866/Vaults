# Interest
To be friendly, show genuine interest in the other person—their hobbies, job, problems, and so on.

Most people care most about themselves. You don’t need to seem interesting. You don’t need rare hobbies or impressive stories. What matters is being able to talk about _their_ interests.

That means you need some knowledge of their topic so you can ask good questions and keep the conversation going.

# Smiling
Smiling makes you more pleasant to others, and your mood can rub off on them. But a fake smile is easy to spot. At minimum, aim for a neutral resting face.

Smiling can also improve your mood. Forcing a smile can make you feel happier, which in turn makes the smile more genuine.

If you’re worried a smile looks strange, remember your resting face already appears tense or unfriendly. What feels like a big smile to you often looks neutral to others.

With practice, you’ll build muscle memory for a relaxed, neutral expression. From there, you can work toward a natural, comfortable smile.
